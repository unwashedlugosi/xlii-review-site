1977-02-26
THE SWING AUDITORIUM IN SAN BERNARDINO
287w
FEBRUARY 26, 1977 — THE SWING AUDITORIUM IN SAN BERNARDINO. A 10,000-SEAT BARN BUILT IN 1949 ON THE NATIONAL ORANGE SHOW FAIRGROUNDS, NAMED AFTER STATE SENATOR RALPH E. SWING. THE ROLLING STONES MADE THEIR AMERICAN CONCERT DEBUT HERE IN 1964. BY 1977 IT WAS CONSISTENTLY DESCRIBED AS A DUMP — FLAT FLOOR WHERE YOU COULDN'T SEE IF ANYONE STOOD UP, NO AIR CONDITIONING IN THE INLAND EMPIRE, AND A STAGEHAND NAMED STUMPY MANAGING THE STAGE. SAN BERNARDINO SAT SIXTY MILES FROM DOWNTOWN LOS ANGELES IN WHAT WAS STILL CITRUS COUNTRY — THE FAIRGROUNDS WERE LITERALLY NAMED FOR THE REGION'S ORANGE INDUSTRY. SEVEN DOLLARS AND FIFTY CENTS, GENERAL ADMISSION. AN EYEWITNESS IN THE FRONT ROW NOTICED MORE HELLS ANGELS THAN HE'D SEEN AT ANY OTHER DEAD SHOW, "BECAUSE IT WAS SAN BERNARDINO." TONIGHT IS THE FIRST SHOW OF 1977, AND THE BAND OPENS WITH THE LIVE DEBUT OF TERRAPIN STATION. IT ARRIVES FULLY FORMED — ONE REVIEWER WROTE THAT THE BAND "WASTES NO EFFORT ON TRYING TO FIGURE THIS TUNE OUT FROM THE STAGE. IT FIRES AT NEAR FULL STRENGTH IMMEDIATELY." ESTIMATED PROPHET DEBUTS THREE SONGS LATER. ROBERT HUNTER, WRITING TO GARCIA AFTER HIS DEATH, RECALLED THAT THEY HAD THE VISION FOR TERRAPIN INDEPENDENTLY, AT THE SAME TIME: "THE FIRST NIGHT IT WAS PLAYED TOOK ME ABOUT AS CLOSE AS I EVER EXPECT TO GET TO FEELING CERTAIN WE WERE DOING WHAT WE WERE PUT HERE TO DO." IN THE FRONT ROW, STEVE PARISH PLUGGED GARCIA'S GUITAR INTO KEITH'S MOOG SYNTHESIZER FOR THE NEW ENVELOPE FILTER SOUND — THE FIRST USE OF THE MU-TRON THAT WOULD DEFINE THE 1977 SOUND. A PLANE CRASHED INTO THE BUILDING IN 1981 AND IT WAS RAZED. RELEASED AS DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 29.
1977-03-18
WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO
259w
MARCH 18, 1977 — WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO. THE HOME COURT — A CONVERTED ICE RINK AT POST AND STEINER IN THE FILLMORE DISTRICT, WHERE TEMPORARY PLYWOOD FLOORING COVERED THE ICE AND YOU COULD LITERALLY BOUNCE ON THE FLOOR. THE DEAD'S SKULL-AND-ROSES BANNER HUNG PERMANENTLY IN THE RAFTERS. BOB WEIR CALLED IT "AN ACOUSTICAL SNAKEPIT." ONE REGULAR DESCRIBED IT AS "A DUMP — AH, BUT WHAT A DUMP." THREE WEEKS AFTER SAN BERNARDINO, THE BAND IS BACK FOR THREE NIGHTS AND TONIGHT THEY DEBUT FIRE ON THE MOUNTAIN — NOT AS A STANDALONE SONG BUT ALREADY PAIRED WITH SCARLET BEGONIAS. IT EMERGED "HESITANTLY, UNEXPECTEDLY, OUT OF SCARLET" — AN ELEVEN-MINUTE FIRST ATTEMPT THAT WOULD GO ON TO BE PAIRED APPROXIMATELY 254 MORE TIMES THROUGH JULY 1995. FIRE ON THE MOUNTAIN NEVER KNEW LIFE WITHOUT ITS PARTNER. THE SUGAREE IS EXCEPTIONAL — TOWARD THE END, THE BAND ENTERS A JAM WHERE KEITH TAKES A SOLO AND THE DRUMS, PIANO, AND BASS INTERACTION BECOMES ITS OWN CONVERSATION. ONE LONGTIME COLLECTOR WITH THIRTY-SIX YEARS OF FOLLOWING THE BAND CALLED THIS HIS PERSONAL FAVORITE SUGAREE, PEGGY-O, AND FIRE ON THE MOUNTAIN AFTER FOUR MONTHS OF COMPARING VERSIONS. PHIL LESH WAS "PLAYING LEAD BASS ABOUT NINETY PERCENT OF THE TIME" — FAR MORE ACTIVE THAN USUAL, AND THE CATALYST THAT GOT THE WHOLE BAND HITTING ABOVE THEIR WEIGHT. THE NOT FADE AWAY BUILDS THROUGH MULTIPLE CHAPTERS, FROM A WAY-BACK GROOVE TO A VIOLA LEE-LIKE CRESCENDO, TO A BEAUTIFULLY QUIET ENDING THAT REVEALS THE FIRST NOTES OF SAINT STEPHEN "LIKE A SUNRISE ON THE BEACH."
1977-03-19
WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO
227w
MARCH 19, 1977 — WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO. NIGHT TWO OF THREE. THE PREVIOUS NIGHT'S SCARLET > FIRE DEBUT WAS STILL RESONATING, BUT TONIGHT THE BAND HAS OTHER IDEAS. THEY PLAY THE ONLY KNOWN EXTENDED VERSION OF TERRAPIN STATION — BEYOND THE USUAL LADY WITH A FAN AND TERRAPIN SECTIONS, THEY VENTURE INTO THE TRANSIT AND AT A SIDING MOVEMENTS THAT EXIST ON THE STUDIO ALBUM BUT WERE ALMOST NEVER ATTEMPTED LIVE. ONE REVIEWER CALLED IT "THE ALHAMBRA" — A BARELY RECOGNIZABLE TERRAPIN TRANSIT FOLLOWED BY AT A SIDING, MAKING THIS THE ONLY CONCERT WHERE THE BAND PLAYED MORE THAN THE STANDARD LIVE ARRANGEMENT. IT FLOWS INTO PLAYING IN THE BAND > SAMSON AND DELILAH > PLAYING IN THE BAND TO CLOSE THE FIRST SET — A FORTY-MINUTE ARC. THE SECOND SET IS ONE UNBROKEN PIECE: EYES OF THE WORLD DISSOLVING INTO DANCING IN THE STREET, WHARF RAT, FRANKLIN'S TOWER, AND SUGAR MAGNOLIA WITHOUT A BREAK BETWEEN THEM. THE DOUBLE ENCORE — ONE MORE SATURDAY NIGHT AND UNCLE JOHN'S BAND — PUTS A BOW ON IT. ONE FAN WHO DISCOVERED THE SHOW AFTER DECADES OF COLLECTING WROTE: "ONCE I HEARD THIS SHOW I FORGOT ABOUT 3/18 AND ITS FIRST-EVER SCARLET > FIRE ALMOST ENTIRELY." THE EAST COAST TOUR GETS ALL THE ATTENTION, BUT THE HOMETOWN WINTERLAND SHOWS FROM THIS YEAR HOLD THEIR OWN AGAINST ANYTHING FROM MAY.
1977-04-22
THE SPECTRUM IN PHILADELPHIA
208w
APRIL 22, 1977 — THE SPECTRUM IN PHILADELPHIA. THE HOUSE THAT ROCK BUILT — AN 18,000-SEAT ARENA IN SOUTH PHILADELPHIA WHERE THE DEAD PLAYED MORE SHOWS THAN ANY OTHER ACT, INCLUDING SPRINGSTEEN. THE BUILDER RAN OUT OF MONEY DURING CONSTRUCTION AND GOT A CODE VARIANCE ON THE ROOF. IT LEAKED FROM DAY ONE. FIVE MONTHS AFTER OPENING, THE WIND BLEW PART OF THE ROOF OFF DURING ICE CAPADES. TWO WEEKS LATER IT BLEW OFF AGAIN. JOHNNY CARSON MADE JOKES ABOUT IT ON THE TONIGHT SHOW. THE SPECTRUM WAS TECHNICALLY BANKRUPT UNTIL 1971. NONE OF THIS MATTERED ONCE THE LIGHTS WENT DOWN. TONIGHT IS THE OPENING NIGHT OF THE SPRING 1977 NORTHEAST RUN — TWENTY-SIX DATES OVER FIVE WEEKS. PHILLY IN 1977 WAS ROCKY COUNTRY. THE MOVIE HAD COME OUT FIVE MONTHS EARLIER AND THE ART MUSEUM STEPS WERE ALREADY A PILGRIMAGE SITE. WMMR WAS ONE OF THE BEST ROCK STATIONS IN THE COUNTRY. CHEESESTEAKS WERE A DOLLAR FIFTY. THE PERFORMANCES WERE DESCRIBED AS "FEISTY, OFTEN AGGRESSIVE" — A BAND "DIGGING THEIR TEETH INTO THE JAMS." THE DANCING IN THE STREETS TAKES A DETOUR INTO I GOT MY MOJO WORKIN' — A ONE-TIME-ONLY MOVE THAT NEVER HAPPENED BEFORE OR AFTER THIS NIGHT. NO ENCORE. THE BAND DIDN'T NEED ONE.
1977-04-23
THE SPRINGFIELD CIVIC CENTER IN SPRINGFIELD, MASSACHUSETTS
181w
APRIL 23, 1977 — THE SPRINGFIELD CIVIC CENTER IN SPRINGFIELD, MASSACHUSETTS. THE BIRTHPLACE OF BASKETBALL — JAMES NAISMITH INVENTED THE GAME HERE IN 1891 AND THE HALL OF FAME IS DOWN THE ROAD. THE CIVIC CENTER WAS A BRUTALIST CONCRETE BOX FROM 1972, ABOUT 9,000 SEATS, BUILT FOR THE BLUE-COLLAR CITY IN THE CONNECTICUT VALLEY. WESTERN MASSACHUSETTS DEADHEADS RELIED ON THIS VENUE — BOSTON WAS TOO FAR FOR AN EASY WEEKNIGHT DRIVE. SECOND DATE OF THE SPRING TOUR. THE BAND HAD PLAYED PHILLY LAST NIGHT AND DRIVEN NORTH THROUGH CONNECTICUT TO GET HERE. ONE WRITER NOTED THIS SHOW "REWARDS A SHARP FOCUS ON THE DETAILS" — THE KIND OF NIGHT WHERE NOTHING SPECTACULAR HAPPENS BUT EVERYTHING IS PLAYED WITH UNCOMMON PRECISION. THE SUGAREE BUILDS SLOWLY AND DELIBERATELY. THE ESTIMATED PROPHET IS STILL FINDING ITS LEGS — ONLY A DOZEN PERFORMANCES OLD — AND YOU CAN HEAR THE BAND EXPLORING WHAT THE SONG CAN BECOME. FIVE MONTHS FROM NOW, NAISMITH'S HOMETOWN WOULD HOST ANOTHER DEAD SHOW ON THE WAY HOME FROM THE FALL TOUR. RELEASED AS DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 53 (BONUS TRACKS).
1977-04-25
THE CAPITOL THEATRE IN PASSAIC, NEW JERSEY
201w
APRIL 25, 1977 — THE CAPITOL THEATRE IN PASSAIC, NEW JERSEY. FIRST OF THREE NIGHTS. THE CAPITOL WAS A 3,140-SEAT MOVIE PALACE FROM 1926 THAT BY THE EARLY SEVENTIES HAD DEGRADED INTO A PORN THEATER. YOUNG PROMOTER JOHN SCHER NEGOTIATED AN ARRANGEMENT WITH THE PORN DISTRIBUTOR — KUNG FU MOVIES BY DAY, ROCK CONCERTS BY NIGHT. HE HIRED THE CREW FROM THE RECENTLY CLOSED FILLMORE EAST AND INSTALLED AN IN-HOUSE VIDEO SYSTEM THAT PRODUCED HIGH-QUALITY BOOTLEG FOOTAGE FOR YEARS. THE ORIGINAL ORNATE PLASTERWORK WAS STRIPPED AND PAINTED DULL OFF-WHITE, BUT THE ACOUSTICS WERE "SUPER" — A SINGLE-FLOOR STADIUM CONFIGURATION THAT ROSE FROM STAGE TO BACK WALL. THE ROLLING STONES, SPRINGSTEEN, AND THE WHO ALL PLAYED HERE. TWO SHOWS A NIGHT ON WEEKENDS MEANT SIX THOUSAND PEOPLE THROUGH AN INTIMATE ROOM IN A SINGLE WEEKEND. TONIGHT THE SCARLET > FIRE IS ONLY THE FOURTH TIME THE SONGS HAVE BEEN PAIRED, AND THE TRANSITION IS "VERY DIFFERENT FROM WHAT IT WOULD BECOME EVEN A COUPLE OF WEEKS LATER" — THE BAND STILL DISCOVERING WHAT THE COMBINATION CAN DO. THREE NIGHTS HERE BEFORE FIVE AT THE PALLADIUM ACROSS THE HUDSON. THE BUILDING WAS DEMOLISHED IN APRIL 1991. A SHOPPING CENTER STANDS ON THE SITE.
1977-04-27
THE CAPITOL THEATRE IN PASSAIC, NEW JERSEY
180w
APRIL 27, 1977 — THE CAPITOL THEATRE IN PASSAIC, NEW JERSEY. THIRD AND FINAL NIGHT. THE BAND HAS BEEN IN THE NEW YORK AREA FOR THREE DAYS, PLAYING THE SAME INTIMATE 3,140-SEAT ROOM WHERE JOHN SCHER'S CREW — HIRED FROM THE DEFUNCT FILLMORE EAST — RAN A PROFESSIONAL OPERATION BEHIND A SHABBY FACADE. TONIGHT'S CENTERPIECE: A TERRAPIN STATION THAT FLOWS DIRECTLY INTO MORNING DEW — ONE OF ONLY FIVE MORNING DEWS THE BAND PLAYED IN ALL OF 1977. THE RARITY ALONE MAKES THIS TAPE ESSENTIAL. THE PASSAIC MORNING DEW IS CONSIDERED "RIGHT UP THERE WITH CORNELL'S" — THE KIND OF VERSION WHERE GARCIA'S VOICE REACHES FOR SOMETHING HE CAN'T QUITE PUT INTO WORDS AND THE GUITAR CARRIES THE REST. TERRAPIN STATION WAS STILL LESS THAN TWO MONTHS OLD AS A LIVE PIECE AND THE BAND WAS PLAYING IT WITH THE INTENSITY OF DISCOVERY — EVERY PERFORMANCE SLIGHTLY DIFFERENT AS THEY WORKED OUT WHERE THE SONG WANTED TO GO. TOMORROW THE TOUR TAKES A DAY OFF. FRIDAY THEY CROSS THE RIVER TO THE PALLADIUM FOR FIVE NIGHTS IN NEW YORK CITY.
1977-04-29
THE PALLADIUM ON EAST 14TH STREET, NEW YORK CITY
176w
APRIL 29, 1977 — THE PALLADIUM ON EAST 14TH STREET, NEW YORK CITY. FIRST OF FIVE NIGHTS. THE PALLADIUM WAS A 3,400-SEAT MOVIE PALACE FROM 1926, DESIGNED BY THOMAS W. LAMB FOR WILLIAM FOX — RECHRISTENED AND REOPENED IN SEPTEMBER 1976 WITH THE BAND. THE ORIGINAL ARCHITECTURAL DETAILS WERE STILL VISIBLE ON THE CEILING BUT GRIMY. KUNG FU MOVIES RAN BY DAY. THREE OR FOUR EVENINGS A WEEK IT BECAME A ROCK VENUE WHERE THE RAMONES, TELEVISION, PATTI SMITH, AND THE DEAD ALL PLAYED ON DIFFERENT NIGHTS — THE ONLY ROOM IN NEW YORK WHERE THOSE WORLDS PASSED THROUGH THE SAME DOORS. NEW YORK IN SPRING 1977: NEAR BANKRUPTCY, TWELVE PERCENT UNEMPLOYMENT, NEIGHBORHOODS HOLLOWED OUT, THE SON OF SAM STALKING THE CITY, STUDIO 54 ABOUT TO OPEN. CBGB WAS FIFTEEN BLOCKS SOUTH ON THE BOWERY. THE WORLD TRADE CENTER TOWERS WERE SIX YEARS OLD AND STILL THE TALLEST BUILDINGS IN THE WORLD. FIVE NIGHTS AT THE PALLADIUM WAS A RESIDENCY, NOT A TOUR STOP. THE DEAD PLAYING SAN FRANCISCO PSYCHEDELIC OPTIMISM IN A CITY COMING APART.
1977-04-30
THE PALLADIUM, NEW YORK CITY
181w
APRIL 30, 1977 — THE PALLADIUM, NEW YORK CITY. SECOND OF FIVE NIGHTS. THE BAND TOOK THE STAGE JUST FIFTEEN MINUTES AFTER THE STATED EIGHT O'CLOCK START — GIVING THE AUDIENCE BARELY ENOUGH TIME TO QUEUE FOR A BEER. FIVE-MINUTE TUNE-UP BREAKS BETWEEN SONGS WERE NOTHING UNUSUAL FOR THE DEAD, BUT UNDERSTANDABLE WHEN SETTLING IN FOR WHAT WOULD BECOME A FIVE-HOUR SHOW. OUTSIDE ON 14TH STREET THE NEIGHBORHOOD WAS A MIX OF PUNK KIDS HEADED DOWN TO THE BOWERY AND DEADHEADS WHO'D FOLLOWED THE TOUR FROM SPRINGFIELD AND PASSAIC. INSIDE, THE THEATER'S STEEP BALCONY AND TIGHT FLOOR PUT EVERYONE CLOSE TO THE STAGE — THIS WAS NOT AN ARENA EXPERIENCE BUT A THEATER SHOW, WITH THE BAND AT EYE LEVEL AND GARCIA'S EXPRESSIONS VISIBLE FROM THE TENTH ROW. THE LAZY LIGHTNIN' > SUPPLICATION IS TIGHT AND THE ESTIMATED PROPHET IS STILL EVOLVING — THE BAND HADN'T SETTLED INTO A FIXED ARRANGEMENT AND EACH PERFORMANCE WAS AN EXPERIMENT. THE PALLADIUM CLOSED IN 1982. IT IS NOW A DORMITORY FOR NEW YORK UNIVERSITY. THE ORNATE LAMB-DESIGNED CEILING IS STILL THERE, ABOVE BUNK BEDS.
1977-05-04
THE PALLADIUM, NEW YORK CITY
154w
MAY 4, 1977 — THE PALLADIUM, NEW YORK CITY. THE LAST OF THE PALLADIUM NIGHTS. FIVE SHOWS IN SEVEN DAYS AT ONE VENUE — A RESIDENCY WHERE THE BAND AND AUDIENCE HAD BUILT SOMETHING TOGETHER OVER THE COURSE OF A WEEK. THE PALLADIUM WAS BIGGER THAN A CLUB BUT MORE INTIMATE THAN AN ARENA, WHICH IS WHY BANDS LOVED IT — YOU COULD FEEL THE ROOM RESPONDING. THE EYES OF THE WORLD IS MONUMENTAL — THE KIND OF EXTENDED EXPLORATION WHERE THE BAND BUILDS SOMETHING OVER FIFTEEN MINUTES AND YOU CAN HEAR EACH MUSICIAN LISTENING TO THE OTHERS. THE WHARF RAT HITS WITH FULL EMOTIONAL WEIGHT. THE NOT FADE AWAY AND UNCLE JOHN'S BAND CLOSE IT OUT WITH AUTHORITY. TOMORROW THE TOUR HEADS NORTH — NEW HAVEN, THEN BOSTON, THEN A CAMPUS GYMNASIUM IN ITHACA WHERE SOMETHING WILL HAPPEN THAT NO ONE IN THIS ROOM TONIGHT COULD PREDICT. RELEASED AS DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 50.
1977-05-05
THE NEW HAVEN COLISEUM IN NEW HAVEN, CONNECTICUT
147w
MAY 5, 1977 — THE NEW HAVEN COLISEUM IN NEW HAVEN, CONNECTICUT. A 10,500-SEAT ARENA BUILT IN 1972 WITH AN INVERTED PYRAMID DESIGN BY ARCHITECT KEVIN ROCHE — THE PARKING GARAGE SAT ON TOP OF THE BUILDING BECAUSE THE HIGH WATER TABLE MADE UNDERGROUND PARKING IMPOSSIBLE. IT LOOKED LIKE A BRUTALIST SPACESHIP HAD LANDED IN DOWNTOWN NEW HAVEN. YALE UNIVERSITY WAS THREE BLOCKS AWAY. THIS IS THE FIRST OF THREE CONSECUTIVE NIGHTS IN THREE DIFFERENT CITIES — NEW HAVEN, BOSTON, CORNELL — THAT WOULD BECOME THE MOST FAMOUS STRETCH OF THE TOUR. "IN SOME RESPECTS, THIS NIGHT IS THE EQUAL OF THOSE LEGENDARY SHOWS" — A VERDICT THAT SAYS EVERYTHING ABOUT HOW CONSISTENTLY THE BAND WAS PLAYING. THE LAZY LIGHTNIN' > SUPPLICATION CRACKLES. THE SAINT STEPHEN MAKES ITS FIRST APPEARANCE OF THE SPRING TOUR. RELEASED AS PART OF THE MAY 1977: GET SHOWN THE LIGHT BOX SET.
1977-05-07
BOSTON GARDEN
225w
MAY 7, 1977 — BOSTON GARDEN. A CRAMPED, SWELTERING ARENA FROM 1928 BUILT BY BOXING PROMOTER TEX RICKARD, SITTING DIRECTLY ABOVE NORTH STATION. NO AIR CONDITIONING. STEEL COLUMNS OBSTRUCTING VIEWS FROM HALF THE SEATS. LOW CEILINGS THAT TRAPPED SOUND AND HEAT UNTIL THE BUILDING FELT LIKE A KETTLE. PEELING PAINT, NARROW HALLWAYS, BATHROOMS THAT WERE LEGENDARY IN THEIR AWFULNESS. THE GARDEN WAS EVERYTHING A MODERN ARENA IS NOT AND DEADHEADS LOVED IT FOR EXACTLY THAT REASON — BOBBY ORR HAD FLOWN THROUGH THIS AIR, BILL RUSSELL HAD RAISED CHAMPIONSHIP BANNERS INTO THESE RAFTERS, AND WHEN PHIL LESH HIT A LOW NOTE THE PLASTER TREMBLED. IT WAS BILL KREUTZMANN'S BIRTHDAY. EQUIPMENT PROBLEMS PLAGUED THE FIRST SET WITH LONG BREAKS BETWEEN SONGS — BUT A FRONT-ROW PHOTOGRAPHER CAUGHT GARCIA BREAKING INTO A BIG GRIN DURING MISSISSIPPI HALF-STEP. THAT HALF-STEP IS ARGUED BY MANY TO BE THE BEST VERSION EVER PLAYED. THE BAND ROSE ABOVE THE TECHNICAL DIFFICULTIES AND THE SECOND SET HIT FULL STRIDE. WBCN — THE LEGENDARY ROCK STATION, CHARLES LAQUIDARA'S MORNING SHOW — HAD BEEN TALKING UP THE SHOW ALL WEEK. BOSTON IN THE SPRING OF 1977 WAS STILL BRUISED FROM THE BUSING CRISIS BUT ITS COLLEGE-TOWN ENERGY WAS UNDIMINISHED. TOMORROW IS CORNELL. RELEASED AS PART OF THE MAY 1977: GET SHOWN THE LIGHT BOX SET AND SEPARATELY ON VINYL FOR RECORD STORE DAY 2023.
1977-05-08
BARTON HALL AT CORNELL UNIVERSITY IN ITHACA, NEW YORK
400w
MAY 8, 1977 — BARTON HALL AT CORNELL UNIVERSITY IN ITHACA, NEW YORK. MOTHER'S DAY. THE MOST FAMOUS GRATEFUL DEAD RECORDING IN EXISTENCE. BARTON HALL IS A 1914 MILITARY DRILL HALL WITH A WOODEN FLOOR — A CAVERNOUS GYMNASIUM ON AN ISOLATED CAMPUS IN THE FINGER LAKES REGION WHERE YOU ARRIVE VIA ROUTE 13 THROUGH ROLLING FARMLAND AND GORGES SWOLLEN WITH SNOWMELT. EARLY MAY IN THE FINGER LAKES: THE APPLE ORCHARDS SHOULD BE IN FULL BLOSSOM AND THE LILACS BLOOMING, BUT 1977 WAS NOT A NORMAL SPRING — THE BRUTAL WINTER HAD LINGERED AND THE TREES WERE ONLY BEGINNING TO LEAF OUT. TICKETS WERE SIX DOLLARS AND FIFTY CENTS. THE CORNELL CONCERT COMMISSION ORGANIZED IT — THEY HAD BEEN $100,000 IN DEBT FROM PREVIOUS MONEY-LOSING SHOWS AND WERE OPERATING UNDER RESTRICTIONS FOLLOWING A 1973 DEEP PURPLE RIOT. FANS DROVE FROM AS FAR AS NORTH CAROLINA. HUNDREDS OF ADDITIONAL PEOPLE WERE ADMITTED BY SINGING, DANCING, TELLING JOKES, OR OFFERING GUITAR PICKS AND PEANUT BUTTER SANDWICHES. THE HALL WAS DENSELY PACKED AND STEAMING HOT — T-SHIRTS CLINGING TO SWEATY BACKS, THE BAND ASKING THE AUDIENCE TO MOVE BACK AND CREATE SPACE. THE SCARLET BEGONIAS > FIRE ON THE MOUNTAIN THAT OPENS THE SECOND SET IS THE VERSION ALL OTHERS ARE MEASURED AGAINST. THE MORNING DEW THAT CLOSES IT — THIRTEEN MINUTES — IS WHAT THE BAND'S ARCHIVIST CALLED "QUITE POSSIBLY ONE OF THE GRATEFUL DEAD'S BEST-EVER LIVE PERFORMANCES," THE BAND "SINGULARLY FOCUSED ON ACHIEVING THE CATHARTIC, FRENZIED RELEASE OF THE CLIMAX." THE ENCORE WAS ONE MORE SATURDAY NIGHT — PLAYED ON A SUNDAY. SOMEONE SAID "THANKS MOM" INTO THE MICROPHONE. WHEN THE AUDIENCE EMERGED FROM THE SWELTERING HALL THEY WALKED INTO A MAY SNOWSTORM — "BIG BILLOWING FLAKES" SWIRLING IN THE PARKING LOT LIGHTS. "ALMOST EVERYONE I'VE SPOKEN TO WHO WENT TO THE CONCERT MENTIONS IT," WROTE PETER CONNERS IN HIS BOOK ABOUT THE SHOW. ONLY THREE PHOTOGRAPHS OF THE CONCERT ARE KNOWN TO EXIST. THE TAPE BECAME THE MOST COPIED CONCERT RECORDING IN HISTORY, PASSED HAND TO HAND THROUGH THE MAIL FOR DECADES BEFORE THE INTERNET EXISTED. IN 2012 THE LIBRARY OF CONGRESS ADDED IT TO THE NATIONAL RECORDING REGISTRY. BOB WEIR, ASKED ABOUT THE SHOW YEARS LATER: "I CAN'T REMEMBER THAT SPECIFICALLY. THE WHOLE TOUR WAS LIKE THAT FOR ME." RELEASED AS A STANDALONE ALBUM IN 2017 AND AS PART OF THE MAY 1977: GET SHOWN THE LIGHT BOX SET.
1977-05-09
THE WAR MEMORIAL AUDITORIUM IN BUFFALO, NEW YORK
207w
MAY 9, 1977 — THE WAR MEMORIAL AUDITORIUM IN BUFFALO, NEW YORK. AN ART DECO BUILDING FROM 1940, BUILT AS A WPA PROJECT WITH NEW DEAL MONEY — BUFF-COLORED BRICK AND LIMESTONE WITH DECORATIVE METALWORK, HIGH CEILINGS, AND THE KIND OF ORNATE DETAILING THAT NO MODERN ARENA CAN REPLICATE. THE ACOUSTICS IN THESE OLDER AUDITORIUMS WERE OFTEN BETTER THAN THE CONCRETE BOWLS THAT REPLACED THEM. BUFFALO IN THE SPRING OF 1977 WAS STILL RECOVERING FROM THE BLIZZARD THAT HAD BURIED THE CITY IN JANUARY — FIVE PEOPLE DEAD, DRIFTS TO SECOND-STORY WINDOWS, THE NATIONAL GUARD CALLED IN. EVEN BY MAY THE TRAUMA WAS FRESH. THE NIGHT AFTER CORNELL, AND SOME COLLECTORS ARGUE THIS SHOW IS BETTER. THE HELP ON THE WAY > SLIPKNOT! > FRANKLIN'S TOWER THAT OPENS THE SECOND SET SPANS OVER THIRTY MINUTES AND IS CONSIDERED BY MANY TO BE THE DEFINITIVE VERSION — TIGHTER AND MORE FEROCIOUS THAN CORNELL'S. THE ENCORE FEATURED "ONE OF THE VERY BEST UNCLE JOHN'S BANDS THE DEAD EVER PLAYED LIVE." SOME CONSIDER THIS SHOW "OVERSHADOWED BY YEARS OF HYPE AND ADORATION FOR THE CORNELL PERFORMANCE." THE BUILDING WAS DEMOLISHED IN 2009 DESPITE EFFORTS TO SAVE IT. RELEASED AS PART OF THE MAY 1977: GET SHOWN THE LIGHT BOX SET.
1977-05-11
THE ST
179w
MAY 11, 1977 — THE ST. PAUL CIVIC CENTER IN ST. PAUL, MINNESOTA. SIXTEEN THOUSAND SEATS IN A BRUTALIST CONCRETE ARENA THAT OPENED IN 1973 AS PART OF A DOWNTOWN REDEVELOPMENT PUSH. THE DEAD HAD CROSSED THE COUNTRY FROM BUFFALO — A DIFFERENT WORLD FROM THE NORTHEAST COLLEGE TOWNS AND MOVIE PALACES THEY'D BEEN PLAYING ALL MONTH. "ON A STEAMY HOT NIGHT, THE DEAD ARE HAVING A HIGH TIME, PLAYING JOKES ON EACH OTHER" — THE KIND OF LOOSE, RELAXED SHOW THAT HAPPENED WHEN THE BAND WAS IN THE MIDDLE OF A RUN AND EVERYTHING FELT EASY. TONIGHT FEATURES THE DEBUT OF PASSENGER — PHIL LESH'S COMPOSITION, SUNG BY WEIR AND DONNA BECAUSE LESH HAD VOCAL CORD DAMAGE. IT WOULD STAY IN THE SETLIST THROUGH 1981 AND THEN DISAPPEAR. ALSO THE FIRST-EVER ESTIMATED PROPHET > EYES OF THE WORLD PAIRING. CHICAGO WAS 400 MILES TOMORROW WAS THE AUDITORIUM THEATRE — ADLER AND SULLIVAN'S MASTERPIECE, ONE OF THE MOST BEAUTIFUL ROOMS IN AMERICA. THE CIVIC CENTER WAS TORN DOWN IN 1998. RELEASED AS PART OF THE MAY 1977 BOX SET.
1977-05-13
THE AUDITORIUM THEATRE IN CHICAGO
161w
MAY 13, 1977 — THE AUDITORIUM THEATRE IN CHICAGO. A NATIONAL HISTORIC LANDMARK BUILT IN 1889 BY DANKMAR ADLER AND LOUIS SULLIVAN — TWO ARCHITECTS WHO INVENTED THE MODERN SKYSCRAPER AND WROTE THE BOOK ON THEATER ACOUSTICS. GOLD LEAF ARCHES, STAINED GLASS, AND AN AUDITORIUM DESIGNED SO THAT EVERY SEAT COULD HEAR A WHISPER FROM THE STAGE. THE DEAD PLAYING HERE WAS LIKE PUTTING A ROCK BAND IN A CATHEDRAL — 3,875 SEATS, EACH ONE WITH PERFECT SIGHTLINES. THE BUILDING HAD BEEN THREATENED WITH DEMOLITION IN THE FIFTIES AND SAVED BY A PRESERVATION CAMPAIGN. CHICAGO IN MAY 1977 WAS IN THE MIDDLE OF AN UNPRECEDENTED HEAT WAVE — NINE CONSECUTIVE DAYS ABOVE NINETY DEGREES, THE WARMEST MAY IN THE CITY'S RECORDED HISTORY. THE MUSIC NEVER STOPPED IS EXPLOSIVE. THE TERRAPIN STATION STILL CRACKLES WITH THE ENERGY OF A NEW SONG — FEWER THAN TWENTY PERFORMANCES OLD. GARCIA IN AN INTERVIEW FROM THIS PERIOD: "WE'RE HAVING FUN AGAIN." YOU CAN HEAR IT.
1977-05-17
COLEMAN COLISEUM AT THE UNIVERSITY OF ALABAMA IN TUSCALOOSA
211w
MAY 17, 1977 — COLEMAN COLISEUM AT THE UNIVERSITY OF ALABAMA IN TUSCALOOSA. DONNA JEAN GODCHAUX'S HOME STATE. THE DEEP SOUTH WAS NOT REGULAR DEAD TERRITORY — ALABAMA DEADHEADS USUALLY HAD TO DRIVE FIVE HOURS TO ATLANTA. THE COLISEUM IS A 15,000-SEAT BASKETBALL ARENA ON BEAR BRYANT'S CAMPUS. TUSCALOOSA SITS ON THE BLACK WARRIOR RIVER, SURROUNDED BY RED CLAY AND PINE FOREST. BY MID-MAY THE HUMIDITY IS RELENTLESS, THE MAGNOLIAS ARE BLOOMING, AND THE FIRST FIREFLIES ARE STARTING. UPSTATE NEW YORK IN EARLY NOVEMBER: THE LEAVES WERE DOWN, LAKE ERIE WAS THE COLDEST ON RECORD FOR THE DATE, AND THE REGION WAS STILL HAUNTED BY THE PREVIOUS WINTER'S BLIZZARD. THE REEL-TO-REEL MASTERS DETERIORATED TOWARD THE END OF THE SHOW — A CASSETTE MASTER HAD TO BE USED TO FILL IN THE GAPS, THOUGH THE SOUND QUALITY REMAINED EXCELLENT. RELEASED AS DICK'S PICKS VOLUME 34.

NOVEMBER 6, 1977 — BROOME COUNTY VETERANS MEMORIAL ARENA IN BINGHAMTON, NEW YORK. THE FALL TOUR CLOSER. A COMPACT CIVIC ARENA IN THE SOUTHERN TIER — ABOUT 7,000 SEATS, SMALL ENOUGH TO FEEL INTIMATE. THE BAND HAD PLAYED A 1,500-SEAT COLLEGE GYM AT COLGATE TWO NIGHTS AGO, A ROWDY ARENA IN ROCHESTER LAST NIGHT, AND NOW THEY CLOSE THE RUN IN BINGHAMTON — THREE SHOWS IN THREE DAYS ACROSS UPSTATE NEW YORK. A DUPREE'S DIAMOND BLUES — A RARITY BY 1977, ONLY RECENTLY RETURNED TO THE SETLIST AFTER THE PORTLAND BREAKOUT. A MISSISSIPPI HALF-STEP THAT OPENS WITH QUIET CONFIDENCE. THE MUSIC NEVER STOPPED HITS WITH EUPHORIC ENERGY — THE BAND KNOWS THE TOUR IS ENDING AND THEY'RE LEAVING NOTHING IN RESERVE. THE TAPE WAS RECORDED BY BETTY CANTOR-JACKSON AND WAS ONLY RECENTLY ACQUIRED AND ENTERED INTO THE VAULT BEFORE THE OFFICIAL RELEASE. RELEASED AS DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 25.

DECEMBER 27, 1977 — WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO. FIRST NIGHT OF THE YEAR-END RUN. SIX WEEKS SINCE BINGHAMTON. THE WORLD HAD SHIFTED: CLOSE ENCOUNTERS OF THE THIRD KIND OPENED DECEMBER 14 — SPIELBERG'S MOTHERSHIP LANDING AT DEVIL'S TOWER. SATURDAY NIGHT FEVER OPENED DECEMBER 16 — JOHN TRAVOLTA IN A WHITE SUIT, THE BEE GEES ON THE SOUNDTRACK, DISCO ASCENDING. DEBBY BOONE'S "YOU LIGHT UP MY LIFE" HAD BEEN NUMBER ONE FOR TEN WEEKS AND SHOWED NO SIGNS OF LEAVING. THE DEAD WERE BACK AT WINTERLAND — THE OLD ICE RINK WHERE THE PLYWOOD FLOOR BOUNCED AND PHIL LESH'S BASS CAUSED PLASTER CHUNKS TO BREAK FREE FROM THE CEILING AND RAIN DOWN ON PEOPLE'S HEADS. DECEMBER IN SAN FRANCISCO: SHORT DAYS ENDING BEFORE FIVE O'CLOCK, THE CALIFORNIA DROUGHT EASING BUT THE HILLS STILL BROWN, FOG ROLLING THROUGH THE FILLMORE DISTRICT. FOUR NIGHTS TO CLOSE OUT WHAT THE BAND'S ARCHIVIST WOULD LATER CALL THE GREATEST YEAR IN THEIR HISTORY.

DECEMBER 29, 1977 — WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO. THIRD NIGHT OF THE YEAR-END RUN, AND ONE OF THE MOST CELEBRATED DEAD SHOWS EVER PLAYED. THE HIGHLIGHT: THE RETURN OF CHINA CAT SUNFLOWER > I KNOW YOU RIDER AFTER A THREE-YEAR ABSENCE — NOT PLAYED SINCE OCTOBER 1974. THE AUDIENCE ERUPTION WHEN THE OPENING NOTES HIT IS AUDIBLE ON THE TAPE. A PHOTOGRAPHER NAMED BOB MINKIN HAD TRAVELED SOLO FROM NEW YORK CITY ON CHRISTMAS DAY VIA AMTRAK AND GREYHOUND. HE LOST HIS WALLET — AND ALL HIS TICKETS TO THREE SOLD-OUT SHOWS — AT THE GREYHOUND TERMINAL. A WINTERLAND PRODUCTIONS EMPLOYEE NAMED GLORIA PULIDO REPLACED THEM. MINKIN DESCRIBED WINTERLAND AS "THE LAST OF SAN FRANCISCO'S LEGENDARY VENUES" FOR A YOUNG DEADHEAD WHO NEVER EXPERIENCED THE FILLMORE OR THE AVALON. THE CHINA CAT RETURN WAS A ONE-OFF — IT WOULDN'T APPEAR AGAIN UNTIL FEBRUARY 1979. A TEASE, NOT A COMEBACK. CRITICS CALLED IT "ARGUABLY THE MOST CONSISTENT CONCERT IN THE DICK'S PICKS SERIES" — OPENING WITH "ONE OF THE STRONGEST VERSIONS OF JACK STRAW EXTANT AND NOT LOOKING BACK." RELEASED AS DICK'S PICKS VOLUME 10.
1977-12-30
WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO
174w
DECEMBER 30, 1977 — WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO. THE NIGHT AFTER THE CHINA CAT RETURN. TOMORROW IS NEW YEAR'S EVE. ONE CRITIC WROTE THAT THIS SHOW HAS "A MYSTERIOUS GRACE ALL ITS OWN" — THE BAND PLAYING WITH THE RELAXED CONFIDENCE OF A GROUP THAT HAD JUST PULLED OFF SOMETHING SPECIAL AND KNEW THE YEAR WAS ALMOST OVER. THE ESTIMATED PROPHET > EYES OF THE WORLD > SAINT STEPHEN > SUGAR MAGNOLIA SECOND SET BUILDS WITHOUT FORCING ANYTHING — EACH SONG FLOWING INTO THE NEXT AS IF THE TRANSITIONS WERE INEVITABLE. THE MISSISSIPPI HALF-STEP THAT OPENS THE SHOW IS UNHURRIED AND WARM. THE ROW JIMMY IS GENTLE. THE SHIP OF FOOLS CARRIES REAL WEIGHT. DISCO WAS ABOUT TO SWALLOW AMERICAN MUSIC — SATURDAY NIGHT FEVER WAS TWO WEEKS OLD AND THE BEE GEES WERE EVERYWHERE. HERE WAS THE GRATEFUL DEAD IN A CONVERTED ICE RINK, PLAYING A SHIP OF FOOLS AND AN EYES OF THE WORLD THAT SUGGESTED THEY HAD NO INTEREST IN WHAT THE REST OF THE CULTURE WAS DOING. THEY NEVER HAD.
