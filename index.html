#1Feb 22, 1974Winterland Arena, San Francisco, CA

FEBRUARY 22, 1974 — WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO. THE FIRST SHOW OF 1974, AND THE FIRST OF A THREE-NIGHT RUN AT THE DEAD'S HOME BASE — A CONVERTED ICE RINK WITH PLYWOOD OVER THE SKATING SURFACE AND A CEILING THAT BOB WEIR CALLED "AN ACOUSTICAL SNAKEPIT." THE BAND HADN'T PLAYED IN TWO MONTHS AND THE WALL OF SOUND WASN'T READY YET — ITS PUBLIC DEBUT WAS STILL A MONTH AWAY. BUT THE EARLY VERSION OF THE SYSTEM WAS IN PLACE, AND DURING A SOUNDCHECK THAT WEEKEND, JACK CASADY OF JEFFERSON AIRPLANE WAS FOUND PLAYING PHIL LESH'S BASS RIG WHILE PHIL SAT ALONE IN THE BACK OF THE EMPTY ARENA, LISTENING — HE WANTED TO HEAR WHAT HIS OWN BASS SOUNDED LIKE THROUGH THE NEW SPEAKERS.

THREE SONGS DEBUTED THIS NIGHT: SHIP OF FOOLS, U.S. BLUES, AND IT MUST HAVE BEEN THE ROSES — ALL THREE WOULD APPEAR ON FROM THE MARS HOTEL FOUR MONTHS LATER. THE BAND PLAYED THREE FULL SETS, WITH A THEY LOVE EACH OTHER THAT TURNED OUT TO BE THE LAST-EVER PERFORMANCE OF THE SONG UNTIL IT WAS REVIVED YEARS LATER. SELECTIONS APPEAR AS BONUS TRACKS ON DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 42.

#2Feb 23, 1974Winterland Arena, San Francisco, CA

FEBRUARY 23, 1974 — WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO. NIGHT TWO. THE LAST HERE COMES SUNSHINE — THE BAND PLAYED IT HERE AND THEN GARCIA APPARENTLY FORGOT THE SONG EXISTED, LATER REFERRING TO IT AS SOMETHING THEY NEVER PLAYED LIVE BECAUSE "IT WASN'T A VERY GOOD SONG." IT WASN'T HEARD AGAIN FOR EIGHTEEN YEARS. FANS NOW RATE THIS VERSION AS THE DEFINITIVE ONE.

THE SHOW OPENED WITH AROUND AND AROUND, WHICH WAS NORMALLY A SET CLOSER — AN IMMEDIATE SIGNAL THAT THE BAND CAME TO PLAY. THE SECOND SET CENTERPIECE IS ROUGHLY AN HOUR OF UNBROKEN MUSIC: HE'S GONE DISSOLVING INTO TRUCKIN', THEN DRUMS, THEN A LONG, MOSTLY QUIET OTHER ONE THAT BUILDS SLOWLY WITH EARLY SLIPKNOT RIFFING — THE SKELETON OF A COMPOSITION THAT WOULDN'T FULLY EMERGE UNTIL 1975 — BEFORE OPENING INTO EYES OF THE WORLD. ONE REVIEWER DESCRIBED THE EYES AS "A VAST FIELD OF ELECTRIC FLOWERS AND WARM LIGHT FROM A BRIGHT BLUE SKY."

THE NIGHT ENDED WITH THE FULL THREE-SONG FAREWELL: CASEY JONES, JOHNNY B. GOODE, AND WE BID YOU GOODNIGHT. RELEASED AS DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 42.

#3Feb 24, 1974Winterland Arena, San Francisco, CA

FEBRUARY 24, 1974 — WINTERLAND ARENA, SAN FRANCISCO. THE FINAL NIGHT. BILL GRAHAM TOOK THE MICROPHONE AND TOLD THE CROWD: "WHATEVER IS GOING ON WITH THE REST OF THE WORLD, WHETHER IT BE WARS, OR KIDNAPPING, OR CRIMES, THIS IS A PEACEFUL SUNDAY NIGHT WITH THE GRATEFUL DEAD." PATTY HEARST HAD BEEN KIDNAPPED FROM HER BERKELEY APARTMENT TWENTY DAYS EARLIER. NIXON WAS STILL IN THE WHITE HOUSE. THE WAR IN VIETNAM WAS TECHNICALLY OVER BUT NOBODY BELIEVED IT.

THE DARK STAR THAT DOMINATES THE SECOND SET RUNS TWENTY-NINE MINUTES. THE BAND DIVES INTO INSTRUMENTAL EXPLORATION IMMEDIATELY AND DOESN'T REACH THE FIRST VERSE UNTIL EIGHTEEN MINUTES IN — A JAZZ-DRIVEN, ANGULAR APPROACH THAT WOULD DEFINE THE REST OF THE YEAR. THE MORNING DEW THAT FOLLOWS — A SONG ABOUT NUCLEAR AFTERMATH — LANDS DIFFERENTLY AFTER GRAHAM'S INTRODUCTION ABOUT PEACE IN A VIOLENT WORLD. ONE CRITIC CALLED IT "DEEP, MOURNFUL, POWERFUL AND RELIGIOUS" WITH "A DEVASTATING MIDDLE SOLO THAT'S TRILLED SO HARD IT MUST HAVE GONE INTO TRIPLE-DIGIT DECIBELS."

THE BABY BLUE ENCORE WAS THE FIRST TIME THEY'D PLAYED THE DYLAN SONG SINCE 1972 AND THE LAST TIME UNTIL 1981. AS THE CROWD FILED OUT, PHIL LESH SAID ON MIC: "SEE YA NEXT MONTH AT THE COW PALACE, I HOPE." RELEASED AS DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 13.

#4Mar 23, 1974Cow Palace, Daly City, CA

MARCH 23, 1974 — COW PALACE, DALY CITY. THE BUILDING WAS LITERALLY NAMED AFTER COWS — BUILT AS A WPA LIVESTOCK PAVILION DURING THE DEPRESSION, THE NICKNAME CAME FROM A CITY SUPERVISOR WHO CALLED IT "A PALACE FOR COWS." IT HOSTED THE 1964 REPUBLICAN CONVENTION WHERE GOLDWATER DELEGATES BOOED NELSON ROCKEFELLER SO FEROCIOUSLY HE COULDN'T SPEAK. THE BEATLES OPENED THEIR FIRST NORTH AMERICAN TOUR HERE AND TWO THOUSAND FANS RUSHED THE STAGE.

TONIGHT WAS BILLED AS "THE SOUND TEST." TICKETS WERE TWO DOLLARS AND FIFTY CENTS. THE DEAD HAD BOUGHT RADIO TIME ON KFRC AND GIVEN AWAY TICKETS, DELIBERATELY DRAWING AN AUDIENCE THAT HAD NEVER SEEN THEM. THE WALL OF SOUND STOOD THREE STORIES HIGH BEHIND THE BAND, HIDDEN BEHIND DARK CURTAINS FOR THE FIRST SET. BETWEEN SETS THE CURTAINS ROSE AND THE FULL SYSTEM WAS REVEALED. SECONDS INTO THE FIRST SONG, THE SYSTEM FAILED. GARCIA PLAYED CHOPIN'S FUNERAL MARCH WHILE THE CREW FIXED IT. WEIR APOLOGIZED: "A THOUSAND PARDONS, FOLKS."

ALSO THE LIVE DEBUT OF SCARLET BEGONIAS — WHICH WOULD BE IN THE RECORDING STUDIO ONE WEEK LATER — AND CASSIDY, NAMED FOR BOTH A CREW MEMBER'S INFANT DAUGHTER AND THE DEAD BEAT HERO NEAL CASSADY, WHO'D DIED NEAR A RAILROAD TRACK IN MEXICO SIX YEARS EARLIER. CASSIDY WOULDN'T BE PLAYED AGAIN UNTIL 1976. ONE FAN SPENT THE ENTIRE SHOW SEATED FACING THE REAR WALL AND DIDN'T REALIZE IT UNTIL GRAHAM TURNED ON THE HOUSE LIGHTS — THE SOUND HAD COME FROM EVERYWHERE. RELEASED AS DICK'S PICKS VOLUME 24.

#5May 12, 1974University of Nevada, Reno, NV

MAY 12, 1974 — MACKAY STADIUM, UNIVERSITY OF NEVADA, RENO. MOTHER'S DAY. AN OUTDOOR FOOTBALL STADIUM AT FORTY-SIX HUNDRED FEET WITH THE SIERRA NEVADA VISIBLE TO THE WEST. THE FIRST WALL OF SOUND SHOW OUTSIDE THE BAY AREA AND THE START OF THE REAL TOUR.

THE WASHOE ZEPHYR HIT — A SEASONAL WIND THAT BLOWS DOWN OUT OF THE SIERRA WITH ENOUGH FORCE THAT MARK TWAIN ONCE WROTE IT "ROLLS UP TIN ROOFS LIKE SHEET MUSIC." THE 75-TON SPEAKER ARRAY WAS VISIBLY SWAYING. A GROUP OF FIFTEEN-YEAR-OLDS ON WINDOWPANE ACID THOUGHT THE SPEAKERS WERE BLOWING EVERYONE'S HAIR AND DIDN'T REALIZE IT WAS THE WIND UNTIL THEY STARTED COMING DOWN. THE RENO GAZETTE-JOURNAL COVERED THE EVENT WITH BARELY CONCEALED ALARM — THE CONSERVATIVE CITY DIDN'T KNOW WHAT TO MAKE OF THE HIPPIES OR THEIR ENORMOUS SOUND SYSTEM. SECURITY WANTED TO SEARCH THE CROWD.

GARCIA WAS REPORTEDLY UNHAPPY WITH THE PERFORMANCE. AN EYEWITNESS WHO ATTENDED THE SHOW RECALLED HIM SAYING BACKSTAGE THAT THEY PLAYED "SCRAPPY" AND HE WOULDN'T BLAME ANYONE FOR DEMANDING THEIR MONEY BACK. BUT THE TAPE TELLS A DIFFERENT STORY — THE OTHER ONE STRETCHES PAST TWENTY MINUTES WITH THE FIRST MIND LEFT BODY JAM OF 1974, AND THE TRUCKIN' THAT LAUNCHES IT IS WHITE-HOT. INCLUDED IN THE FROM THE MARS HOTEL FIFTIETH ANNIVERSARY DELUXE EDITION AS A COMPLETE PREVIOUSLY UNRELEASED CONCERT.

#6May 14, 1974Adams Field House, University of Montana, Missoula, MT

MAY 14, 1974 — ADAMS FIELD HOUSE, UNIVERSITY OF MONTANA, MISSOULA. THE ONLY GRATEFUL DEAD SHOW EVER PLAYED IN THE STATE OF MONTANA. IT COST TWENTY THOUSAND DOLLARS TO GET THEM THERE. THE CONNECTION WAS ED MCCLANAHAN, A MERRY PRANKSTER AND PLAYBOY CONTRIBUTOR WHO WAS TEACHING AT THE UNIVERSITY — HE'D PUBLISHED "GRATEFUL DEAD I HAVE KNOWN" IN PLAYBOY TWO YEARS EARLIER.

SEVEN THOUSAND PEOPLE PACKED A FIELD HOUSE WITH MASSIVE LAMINATED WOOD ARCHES AND A FLOOR THAT WAS ORIGINALLY DIRT. ADMISSION WAS SIX DOLLARS. IT SNOWED. THE CAMPUS AD FOR THE SHOW QUOTED THE BAND'S CONTRACT: "ARTIST'S REPUTATION WILL BE SUBSTANTIALLY AND MATERIALLY DAMAGED IF ARTIST IS PREVENTED FROM PERFORMING FOR SAID FULL FIVE HOURS." THE MISSOULIAN SENT A REVIEWER WHO WROTE THAT GARCIA "TOOK A FORAY INTO ROCK, JAZZ AND FINALLY AN ELECTRONIC NEVERNESS THAT LEFT THE CROWD SPELLBOUND" — BUT CALLED THE DARK STAR "ONE INCOHERENT JAM" THAT "RAMBLED ON FOR FORTY MINUTES." HE ALSO WROTE: "GARCIA IS ONE OF THE BEST MUSICIANS THERE IS."

THE SAME DAY WAS THE ANNUAL ABER DAY KEGGER — EIGHT THOUSAND PEOPLE ON A HILLSIDE WITH UNLIMITED BEER. BEFORE THE ENCORE, SOMEONE ARMED WITH A PLASTIC KEGGER PITCHER THREW IT AT THE STAGE AND HIT WEIR IN THE HEAD. WHEN THE BAND CAME BACK, YOU CAN HEAR WEIR SAY: "THANKS A HELL OF A LOT." THE MISSOULIAN EDITORIALIZED: "DRUNK COWBOYS DO NOT BELONG IN A GRATEFUL DEAD CONCERT." RELEASED AS DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 9.

#7May 17, 1974P.N.E. Coliseum, Vancouver, BC, Canada

MAY 17, 1974 — PACIFIC COLISEUM, VANCOUVER. THE ARENA WAS BUILT IN 1968 SPECIFICALLY TO ATTRACT AN NHL FRANCHISE — IT WORKED, AND THE CANUCKS PLAYED THEIR FIRST TWENTY-FIVE SEASONS HERE. IT SITS ON THE GROUNDS OF THE PACIFIC NATIONAL EXHIBITION, VANCOUVER'S ANNUAL SUMMER FAIR, SURROUNDED BY A HORSE TRACK, AN AMUSEMENT PARK, AND A FARMER'S MARKET. THE DEAD HAD PLAYED THREE DIFFERENT BUILDINGS ON THESE GROUNDS SINCE 1966.

THIS WAS JUST THE FOURTH SHOW WITH THE COMPLETE WALL OF SOUND, AND THE BAND'S ONLY 1974 VISIT TO CANADA. KEITH GODCHAUX'S FENDER RHODES CAME INTO ITS OWN HERE — REVIEWERS NOTED "AGGRESSIVE, SPOOKY, ATMOSPHERIC" KEYBOARD WORK THAT SURPASSED HIS EARLIER PLAYING. PHIL LESH GENERATED WHAT ONE LISTENER CALLED "TREMENDOUS RESONANCE" THROUGH THE WALL'S DEDICATED BASS COLUMNS. LESH WOULD LATER DESCRIBE THE EXPERIENCE OF PLAYING THROUGH THE SYSTEM AS "PILOTING A FLYING SAUCER. OR RIDING YOUR OWN SOUND WAVE."

MONEY MONEY MADE ITS FIRST APPEARANCE — ONE OF ONLY THREE PERFORMANCES BEFORE THE SONG WAS PERMANENTLY BURIED. THE VERSION HERE IS THE ONE USED ON THE FROM THE MARS HOTEL ALBUM. PART OF THE PACIFIC NORTHWEST '73-'74 BOX SET — A NINETEEN-CD SET IN A FIRST NATIONS ART BOX BY ROY HENRY VICKERS, NOMINATED FOR A GRAMMY.

#8May 19, 1974Portland Memorial Coliseum, Portland, OR

MAY 19, 1974 — PORTLAND MEMORIAL COLISEUM. THE BUILDING STANDS ON JUST FOUR CONCRETE PIERS DESPITE BEING DOUBLE THE SIZE OF A CITY BLOCK. EIGHTY THOUSAND SQUARE FEET OF GLASS WINDOWS WRAP THE EXTERIOR — WHEN THE BLACKOUT CURTAINS ARE OPEN, IT MAY BE THE ONLY ARENA IN THE WORLD WITH A THREE-HUNDRED-AND-SIXTY-DEGREE VIEW TO THE OUTSIDE. THE BEATLES PLAYED HERE IN 1965 AND ALLEN GINSBERG ATTENDED BOTH SHOWS AND WROTE A POEM ABOUT THE CROWD. THE TRAIL BLAZERS WON A CHAMPIONSHIP ON THIS FLOOR.

THE OREGONIAN NOTED "THOUSANDS OF FANS BRAVING THE PORTLAND DRIZZLE" TO GET IN. THE BAND TOOK THE STAGE FIFTEEN MINUTES EARLY. DAVID LEMIEUX, THE GRATEFUL DEAD'S ARCHIVIST, LATER PLACED THIS SHOW "AMONGST THE BEST DEAD SHOW EVER, UP THERE WITH HARPUR COLLEGE, VENETA, AND CORNELL." DAVID CROSBY SAID ON KPFA THAT IF HE WERE STRANDED ON A DESERT ISLAND, THIS IS THE TAPE HE'D BRING.

THE SECOND SET IS WHY. THE JAM COMING OUT OF TRUCKIN' IS CONSIDERED BY MANY TO BE THE FINEST THE BAND EVER PLAYED — GARCIA ON A DISTORTED TONE, WEIR PLAYING STACCATO CHORDS, THE WHOLE THING BUILDING INTO A MIND LEFT BODY JAM WHERE LESH WANTED TO START NOT FADE AWAY BUT WEIR HAD OTHER IDEAS. THE SBD DIDN'T CIRCULATE UNTIL 1998. PART OF THE PACIFIC NORTHWEST '73-'74 BOX SET.

#9May 21, 1974Edmundson Pavilion, University of Washington, Seattle, WA

MAY 21, 1974 — HEC EDMUNDSON PAVILION, UNIVERSITY OF WASHINGTON, SEATTLE. BUILT IN NINE MONTHS IN 1927 FOR SIX HUNDRED THOUSAND DOLLARS RAISED ALMOST ENTIRELY BY STUDENTS. WHEN IT OPENED IT WAS THE LARGEST ARENA WEST OF THE MISSISSIPPI. NAMED AFTER THE BASKETBALL COACH WHO COMPETED IN THE 1912 STOCKHOLM OLYMPICS AND INVENTED THE FAST BREAK. MASSIVE LAMINATED ARCHES AND FORTY THOUSAND SQUARE FEET OF SKYLIGHTS. THE ENTRY WAS CASUAL — NO MAZES, NO HEAVY SECURITY, PARKING TWO HUNDRED FEET FROM THE DOOR.

KAREN JACKSON FORBES, A UW ALUMNA, PLANNED TO LEAVE AT INTERMISSION. SHE STAYED FIVE HOURS AND WENT ON TO ATTEND EVERY DEAD CONCERT SHE COULD: "IT WAS ABSOLUTELY JUST YOU AND THE BAND. HEC ED WAS A REALLY GREAT PLACE TO HAVE IT." THE SEATTLE TIMES WROTE: "EVERYBODY IN THE HALL LAST NIGHT LITERALLY FELT THE MUSIC."

THIS WAS THE LONGEST SINGLE SONG THE GRATEFUL DEAD EVER PLAYED: FORTY-SIX MINUTES AND FIFTY-FIVE SECONDS OF PLAYING IN THE BAND, OPENING THE SECOND SET. DAVID LEMIEUX: "WHEN THE DEAD TOOK THOSE CHANCES AND PLAYED A FORTY-SEVEN-MINUTE VERSION IT INDICATES THEY WERE IN A PRETTY 'ON' SPACE. THEY WOULDN'T HAVE ATTEMPTED SOMETHING LIKE THAT IF THEY WEREN'T FEELING IT." IT WAS ONLY THE FIFTH SHOW OF THE TOURING WALL OF SOUND. ANOTHER ATTENDEE, RANDY BOWLES, LEFT BEFORE THE SHOW ENDED — NOT FROM DISAPPOINTMENT BUT FROM EMOTIONAL SATURATION. PART OF THE PACIFIC NORTHWEST '73-'74 BOX SET.

#10Jun 8, 1974Oakland Coliseum Stadium, Oakland, CA

JUNE 8, 1974 — OAKLAND COLISEUM STADIUM. THE FIRST BILL GRAHAM "DAY ON THE GREEN" — A FORMAT THAT WOULD DEFINE BAY AREA STADIUM CONCERTS FOR THE NEXT TWO DECADES. GRAHAM WAS A GERMAN-BORN JEWISH REFUGEE WHO'D FLED BERLIN AFTER KRISTALLNACHT AND ARRIVED IN NEW YORK VIA THE RED CROSS AT AGE SIX. HE VIEWED THESE SHOWS AS "CULTURAL EVENTS, NOT MERE TRANSACTIONS."

GATES OPENED AT TEN. COMMANDER CODY PLAYED FIRST AND ASKED THE CROWD: "HOW COME I DON'T SEE MORE OF YOU HIPPIES AT BASEBALL GAMES?" — THE A'S WERE THE REIGNING WORLD CHAMPIONS AND THIS WAS THEIR STADIUM. ABOUT THIRTY THOUSAND PEOPLE SPREAD ACROSS THE OUTFIELD GRASS WITH BLANKETS AND PICNICS IN A VENUE THAT HELD SIXTY THOUSAND. IT WAS PASTORAL, UNHURRIED, THE OPPOSITE OF NIGHTTIME ARENA INTENSITY.

THE BEACH BOYS WERE LATE — STUCK IN TRAFFIC FROM SAN FRANCISCO — AND THEIR CREW REFUSED TO USE THE WALL OF SOUND, INSISTING ON THEIR OWN PA. OWSLEY STANLEY WAS SO UPSET HE BROKE HIS ARM FALLING FROM THE SCAFFOLDING WHILE TAKING DOWN THEIR EQUIPMENT. THE DEAD VOLUNTARILY SHORTENED THEIR SET TO ACCOMMODATE THE DELAY. THAT EVENING, GARCIA DROVE TO THE GREAT AMERICAN MUSIC HALL AND PLAYED ANOTHER FULL SHOW WITH MERL SAUNDERS.

ONE FAN DESCRIBED A WAVE OF CLAPPING TRAVELING ACROSS THE STADIUM AT THE SPEED OF SOUND — "PRETTY INTENSE IN AN ALTERED STATE." THE AUD TAPES ARE SO GOOD THEY REGULARLY CIRCULATE MISLABELED AS SOUNDBOARD.

#11Jun 16, 1974Iowa State Fairgrounds, Des Moines, IA

JUNE 16, 1974 — IOWA STATE FAIRGROUNDS, DES MOINES. THE STAGE SAT IN THE CENTER OF THE RACETRACK WITH THE WALL OF SOUND AIMED AT THE GRANDSTAND — A 600-FOOT CURVED BRICK STRUCTURE BUILT IN 1927 AS A MEMORIAL TO IOWANS WHO DIED IN THE FIRST WORLD WAR. PHIL LESH TOLD THE CROWD: "WE'RE NOT GOING TO PLAY ALL NIGHT, BUT WE ARE GOING TO PLAY ALL DAY." THEY WENT FROM 12:30 TO ABOUT 5:30 IN THE AFTERNOON SUN.

ONE DETAIL THAT DOESN'T SURVIVE ON TAPE: PHIL'S BASS THROUGH THE WALL OF SOUND COULD BE STEERED SPATIALLY — AN ATTENDEE DESCRIBED THE SENSATION OF THE LOW END MOVING FROM SIDE TO SIDE, UP AND DOWN, DURING THE LONG JAMS AS "PISS-YOUR-PANTS EXCITING." BILL GRAHAM WAS THERE, KNEELING AT THE FRONT OF THE STAGE. BEHIND THE STAGE, ELABORATE TENTS SERVED WHAT ONE FAN CALLED "APPARENTLY AMAZING DELICACIES." THE EQUIPMENT METERS GLOWED WITH LITTLE GREEN LIGHTS "PRODUCING ENERGY IN SOMETHING OTHER THAN THE NORMAL VISUAL SPECTRUM."

THREE FULL SETS ON A BEAUTIFUL, SUNNY, NOT-TOO-HOT IOWA AFTERNOON. PHIL HAD A BEARD; JERRY DIDN'T. RELEASED ON ROAD TRIPS VOLUME 2 NUMBER 3.

#12Jun 18, 1974Freedom Hall, Louisville, KY

JUNE 18, 1974 — FREEDOM HALL, LOUISVILLE. BUILT IN 1956 FOR THE WORLD'S CHAMPIONSHIP HORSE SHOW — THE ARENA FLOOR WAS DESIGNED TO ACCOMMODATE A THREE-HUNDRED-FOOT SHOW RING WHERE SADDLEBREDS PARADED IN FIVE GAITS. TONIGHT IT HELD THE GRATEFUL DEAD. IT WAS BILLED AS "FOUR HOURS WITH THE GRATEFUL DEAD." YOU COULD WALK UP AND BUY TICKETS.

DURING BEAT IT ON DOWN THE LINE, GARCIA DROPPED A FEW BARS OF "MY OLD KENTUCKY HOME" — STEPHEN FOSTER'S SONG, PLAYED BEFORE EVERY KENTUCKY DERBY SINCE 1921. SOMEBODY THREW A BOTTLE TOWARD THE STAGE. WEIR ASKED THE CROWD TO PASS BOTTLES FORWARD RATHER THAN THROW THEM. PHIL LESH ADDED: "YEAH, WE COULD USE THE THREE CENTS." THEN A WOMAN SCREAMED — AND LESH DROPPED THE ATOM-BOMB OPENING NOTE OF THE OTHER ONE. WHAT FOLLOWED WAS THE ONLY "IT'S A SIN" JAM IN ALL OF 1974, LAST HEARD IN AUGUST 1969.

LOUISVILLE WAS A BORDER CITY — BOURBON AND HORSE RACING TO ITS BONES, SITTING ON THE OHIO RIVER ACROSS FROM INDIANA. MUHAMMAD ALI WAS BORN HERE. THE MORNING DEW ENCORE WAS ONE OF ONLY THREE TIMES THE DEAD EVER CLOSED A SHOW WITH THAT SONG. RELEASED ON ROAD TRIPS VOLUME 2 NUMBER 3.

#13Jun 20, 1974The Omni, Atlanta, GA

JUNE 20, 1974 — ATLANTA. THE VENUE IS DISPUTED — DEADBASE SAYS THE MUNICIPAL AUDITORIUM; A TICKET STUB CONFIRMS THE OMNI. BOTH WERE IN ATLANTA, AND BOTH WERE THE KIND OF PLACE WHERE THINGS WENT WRONG: THE OMNI WAS CLAD IN COR-TEN STEEL DESIGNED TO RUST TO A PROTECTIVE PATINA, BUT IN ATLANTA'S HUMIDITY IT JUST KEPT CORRODING UNTIL YOU COULD CLIMB THROUGH THE HOLES IN THE WALLS.

MARIA MULDAUR OPENED WITH "MIDNIGHT AT THE OASIS." DURING THE BREAK, THE AMPS BLEW UP — SMOKE AND SPARKS ON STAGE LEFT. SLIPKNOT! WAS BORN AT THIS SHOW — THE MUSICAL IDEA THAT WOULD BECOME THE CENTER OF HELP ON THE WAY > SLIPKNOT! > FRANKLIN'S TOWER. IT EMERGED ORGANICALLY FROM AN EYES OF THE WORLD JAM AND YOU CAN HEAR IT TAKING SHAPE IN REAL TIME. WHEN THE BAND FINISHED, PHIL LESH SAID "THANK YOU AND GOODNIGHT" WITH SUCH FINALITY THAT NO ONE ASKED FOR AN ENCORE. THEY LIT THE DISCO BALL FOR SUGAR MAGNOLIA.

JUNE 22, 1974 — JAI-ALAI FRONTON, MIAMI. JAI ALAI IS THE FASTEST BALL SPORT IN THE WORLD — PLAYERS HURL A HARD RUBBER BALL AT A GRANITE WALL AT A HUNDRED AND FIFTY MILES PER HOUR WHILE GAMBLERS BET IN TIERED SEATS BEHIND A SCREEN. IN 1974, SOUTH FLORIDA HAD NO MAJOR SPORTS ARENAS, SO THE FRONTONS — PURPOSE-BUILT GAMBLING FACILITIES FOR A BASQUE SPORT — DOUBLED AS CONCERT HALLS. SINATRA, HENDRIX, SPRINGSTEEN, AND BOB MARLEY ALL PLAYED FRONTONS. IT WAS SIMPLY WHERE YOU PLAYED IF YOU WERE A MAJOR ACT COMING THROUGH MIAMI.

THE SOUNDCHECK WAS CAPTURED ON TAPE AND IT'S WORTH HEARING: THE BAND WANDERS THROUGH A NOBODY'S JAM INTO THE OTHER ONE INTO A TIGER THAT STRETCHES FOR TWENTY-FIVE MINUTES. THIRD ROW SEATS WERE AVAILABLE FOR TWENTY DOLLARS. MIAMI IN JUNE OF 1974 WAS SUN, HUMIDITY, ART DECO DECAY, RETIREES, CUBAN EXILES, AND THE FRONTON — PABLO ESCOBAR AND THE COCAINE COWBOYS WERE STILL A FEW YEARS AWAY. NEARLY EIGHTY MINUTES FROM THIS NIGHT APPEAR AS THE BONUS DISC ON DAVE'S PICKS VOLUME 34.

JUNE 23, 1974 — JAI-ALAI FRONTON, MIAMI. NIGHT TWO. ONE FAN FLEW IN ON A CARGO PLANE FROM PUERTO RICO THROUGH THE BERMUDA TRIANGLE — THEY STRAPPED INTO SURPLUS GREYHOUND BUS SEATS BOLTED TO THE DECK BEHIND TEN TONS OF TEXTILES. HE HAD ABOUT THIRTY DOLLARS.

#16Jun 26, 1974Providence Civic Center, Providence, RI

JUNE 26, 1974 — PROVIDENCE CIVIC CENTER. ONE REVIEWER CALLED IT "A HORRID VENUE IN ALMOST ALL WAYS." PROVIDENCE IN 1974 WAS A CITY IN DEEP DECLINE — POPULATION FALLING FASTER THAN ANYWHERE EXCEPT DETROIT, DEPARTMENT STORES CLOSED, BUILDINGS BOARDED UP. RAYMOND PATRIARCA, BOSS OF THE NEW ENGLAND MAFIA, RAN HIS EMPIRE FROM FEDERAL HILL A FEW MILES AWAY. THE DEAD PLAYED A MARATHON WITH THREE SETS AND A SEASTONES INTERMISSION. A SPEAKER BLEW DURING SCARLET BEGONIAS, ADDING A SLIGHT METALLIC REVERB TO THE VOCALS. THE THIRD SET OPENED WITH A BEER BARREL POLKA TEASE BEFORE TRUCKIN' LAUNCHED INTO A SEQUENCE THAT DEADBASE TIMES AT THIRTY-ONE MINUTES AND THIRTY-SEVEN SECONDS — ALL OF IT CATALOGUED UNDER A SINGLE ENTRY BECAUSE THE SONGS WERE INSEPARABLE. IT INCLUDED THE OTHER ONE, A SPANISH JAM, AND A WHARF RAT THAT THE AUDIENCE DIDN'T WANT TO END. RELEASED AS DICK'S PICKS VOLUME 12.

JUNE 28, 1974 — BOSTON GARDEN. AN OLD BARN ABOVE NORTH STATION DESIGNED BY A BOXING PROMOTER WHO WANTED EVERY SEAT CLOSE ENOUGH TO SEE THE SWEAT ON A FIGHTER'S BROW. THERE WAS NO AIR CONDITIONING. THIS WAS THE DEAD'S FIRST SHOW AT BOSTON GARDEN. THEY WOULD PLAY IT TWENTY-TWO MORE TIMES BEFORE THE BUILDING WAS DEMOLISHED IN 1998 AND REPLACED BY A PARKING LOT.
