FEBRUARY 27, 1969 — FILLMORE WEST, SAN FRANCISCO. THE NIGHT THE 23-MINUTE DARK STAR THAT BECAME THE CENTERPIECE OF LIVE/DEAD WAS RECORDED. AN AMPEX 16-TRACK MACHINE — ROCK'S FIRST LIVE MULTITRACK RECORDER — SAT IN A TRUCK PARKED ON THE STREET BECAUSE THE DECK WAS TOO HEAVY TO GET INSIDE THE BUILDING. AMPEX ENGINEER RON WICKERSHAM HAD DESIGNED THE SETUP TO INTRUDE AS LITTLE AS POSSIBLE INTO THE PERFORMANCE. THE FILLMORE WEST WAS THE OLD CAROUSEL BALLROOM, A SWING-ERA DANCE PALACE WITH CEILINGS DRAPED IN SILVER GLITTERY FABRIC "ARRANGED LIKE HUGE UPSIDE-DOWN MUSHROOMS." THREE OR FOUR BANDS SHARED EACH BILL, AND HEADLINERS WERE REQUIRED TO STAY IN THE BUILDING DURING ALL PERFORMANCES. PENTANGLE — THE BRITISH ACOUSTIC FOLK GROUP — OPENED. GARCIA LATER SAID HEARING THEM PLANTED THE SEED FOR THE ACOUSTIC EXPLORATIONS THAT BECAME WORKINGMAN'S DEAD AND AMERICAN BEAUTY. A JUGGLER NAMED JIM RINEHART PERFORMED BETWEEN SETS. RALPH GLEASON REVIEWED THE RUN FOR THE SAN FRANCISCO CHRONICLE, CALLING THE BAND "ABSOLUTELY WONDERFUL" WITH GARCIA'S GUITAR PROVIDING "THE SOFT BUTTER FEELING." ROBERT CHRISTGAU IN THE VILLAGE VOICE CALLED THE ALBUM THAT RESULTED "THE FINEST ROCK IMPROVISATION EVER RECORDED." RELEASED ON LIVE/DEAD (1969) AND FILLMORE WEST 1969: THE COMPLETE RECORDINGS (10-CD BOX SET, LIMITED TO 10,000 COPIES).

FEBRUARY 28, 1969 — FILLMORE WEST, SAN FRANCISCO. DICK LATVALA — THE MAN WHO LATER CURATED THE BAND'S TAPE VAULT — CALLED THIS "THE BEST AND MOST EXCITING GRATEFUL DEAD SHOW EVER. WITHOUT A DOUBT." NONE OF THIS NIGHT'S MATERIAL WAS USED ON LIVE/DEAD, WHICH MAKES IT THE GREATEST DEAD SHOW NEVER RELEASED AS A STANDALONE ALBUM UNTIL THE BOX SET DECADES LATER. THE SECOND SET IS ONE CONTINUOUS PIECE: DARK STAR DISSOLVING INTO ST. STEPHEN, THE ELEVEN, ALLIGATOR, CAUTION, FEEDBACK, AND GOODNIGHT — EVERY SONG MELTING INTO THE NEXT WITHOUT A BREAK. THE DARK STAR FEATURES WHAT COLLECTORS CALL THE "SPUTNIK" PASSAGE — AROUND ELEVEN AND A HALF MINUTES IN, GARCIA CONSCIOUSLY STOPS REPEATING WHAT HE'D PLAYED THE NIGHT BEFORE AND FOR A WHILE DROPS OUT ENTIRELY WHILE WEIR CARRIES THE MUSIC ALONE. DAN HEALY MADE ROUGH MIXES FROM THE 16-TRACKS IN THE EARLY NINETIES FOR THE FROM THE VAULT SERIES, AND THANKS TO LATVALA, COPIES SLIPPED INTO CIRCULATION. WHAT MAKES THIS TAPE MATTER IS NOT ANY SINGLE MOMENT BUT THE ARC — THE BAND STARTING IN ONE PLACE AND ARRIVING SOMEWHERE ELSE WITHOUT ANYONE DECIDING WHERE THEY WERE GOING. RELEASED ON FILLMORE WEST 1969: THE COMPLETE RECORDINGS (10-CD BOX SET).

MARCH 1, 1969 — FILLMORE WEST, SAN FRANCISCO. NIGHT THREE OF FOUR, AND THE 16-TRACK MACHINE IN THE TRUCK ON THE STREET WAS STILL ROLLING. ST. STEPHEN WAS THE BAND'S MOST FREQUENTLY PERFORMED SONG IN 1969 — ONLY TURN ON YOUR LOVELIGHT AND DARK STAR WERE PLAYED MORE OFTEN. THE LIVE VERSIONS INCLUDED A SECTION CALLED THE "WILLIAM TELL BRIDGE" THAT SEGUED INTO THE ELEVEN — A PIECE BUILT ON AN 11/8 TIME SIGNATURE, WHICH MEANT ELEVEN BEATS PER MEASURE INSTEAD OF THE STANDARD FOUR. IT WAS A DIRECT PRODUCT OF MICKEY HART'S STUDY OF TABLA DRUMMING AND POLYRHYTHMIC PERCUSSION. DARK STAR, ST. STEPHEN, AND THE ELEVEN HAD BECOME A SINGLE CONTINUOUS PIECE — THREE SONGS FUNCTIONING AS ONE, EACH APPROACHING THE SAME SIGNPOSTS BUT NEVER FOLLOWING THE SAME PATH TWICE. THE FILLMORE FORMAT REQUIRED HEADLINERS TO REMAIN IN-HOUSE DURING ALL PERFORMANCES, WHICH MEANT GARCIA HAD BEEN LISTENING TO PENTANGLE PLAY MULTIPLE SETS ACROSS MULTIPLE NIGHTS. SIR DOUGLAS QUINTET ALSO ON THE BILL — THEY PLAYED "MENDOCINO" AND "SHE'S ABOUT A MOVER" AND GOT EVERYBODY DANCING. DEATH DON'T HAVE NO MERCY FROM THIS RUN ENDED UP ON LIVE/DEAD. RELEASED ON FILLMORE WEST 1969: THE COMPLETE RECORDINGS (10-CD BOX SET).

MARCH 2, 1969 — FILLMORE WEST, SAN FRANCISCO. THE FINAL NIGHT. FOUR SHOWS IN FOUR DAYS, EIGHT SETS, AND THE 16-TRACK MACHINE HAD CAPTURED ALL OF IT. FROM THIS RUN, WARNER BROTHERS SELECTED FIVE SONGS FOR LIVE/DEAD: DARK STAR, ST. STEPHEN, DEATH DON'T HAVE NO MERCY, FEEDBACK, AND AND WE BID YOU GOODNIGHT. THE ALBUM HIT GOLD AND PAID DOWN THE BAND'S DEBT FROM THE EXPENSIVE AOXOMOXOA STUDIO SESSIONS. ROBERT CHRISTGAU GAVE IT AN A-PLUS IN THE NEW YORK TIMES. BLAIR JACKSON REGARDS IT AS THE BEST PSYCHEDELIC ROCK ALBUM OF THE SIXTIES. LENNY KAYE IN ROLLING STONE SAID IT "FORESHADOWED WHERE ROCK IS LIKELY TO BE IN ABOUT FIVE YEARS." THE SEVEN-PIECE LINEUP ON THIS TAPE IS THE BAND AT ITS MOST ADVENTUROUS — TOM CONSTANTEN, WHO HAD INSERTED COMBS, DUTCH DIMES, AND A GYROSCOPE INTO PIANO STRINGS ON THE STUDIO RECORDS, BRINGING HIS MILLS COLLEGE AVANT-GARDE TRAINING INTO A ROCK BAND ALREADY PUSHING PAST WHAT ROCK COULD DO. THE NORTH AMERICAN IBIS ALCHEMICAL COMPANY PROVIDED THE LIGHT SHOW. BILL GRAHAM WOULD CLOSE THE FILLMORE WEST IN JULY 1971. THE BUILDING LATER BECAME A HONDA DEALERSHIP. RELEASED ON LIVE/DEAD AND FILLMORE WEST 1969: THE COMPLETE RECORDINGS.

MAY 23, 1969 — SEMINOLE INDIAN VILLAGE, HOLLYWOOD, FLORIDA. THE BIG ROCK POW WOW — A THREE-DAY FESTIVAL ON SOVEREIGN SEMINOLE LAND, ADJACENT TO OKALEE VILLAGE, THE TOURIST ATTRACTION FAMOUS FOR ALLIGATOR WRESTLING. NO POLICE, NO CONVENTIONAL SECURITY — FEDERAL LAW ENFORCEMENT HAD NO JURISDICTION ON THE RESERVATION. OFFICERS STATIONED THEMSELVES OUTSIDE THE BORDERS AND SEARCHED ANYONE WHO LEFT. TIMOTHY LEARY CHANTED "TURN ON, TUNE IN, DROP OUT" FROM THE STAGE. ORANGE SUNSHINE LSD WAS REPORTEDLY DISTRIBUTED THROUGH THE ORANGE JUICE. SEMINOLE DANCING AND CHANTS HAPPENED ONSTAGE AND OFF THROUGHOUT THE FESTIVAL, BLENDING INDIGENOUS CULTURE WITH PSYCHEDELIC ROCK IN A WAY THAT EXISTED NOWHERE ELSE. ONLY A FEW THOUSAND ATTENDED — THREE MONTHS BEFORE WOODSTOCK, THIS INTIMATE SCALE WAS STILL POSSIBLE. MUDDY WATERS AND JOHNNY WINTER WERE ON THE BILL. THE DEAD PLAYED FRIDAY AND SATURDAY. OWSLEY "BEAR" STANLEY RECORDED BOTH NIGHTS PROFESSIONALLY — HIS TAPES EVENTUALLY BECAME ROAD TRIPS VOLUME 4 NUMBER 1, SUBTITLED "BIG ROCK POW-WOW '69," A 3-CD SET CONTAINING COMPLETE PERFORMANCES FROM BOTH NIGHTS. THIS WAS THE EIGHTH ROCK FESTIVAL OF 1969 — A YEAR THAT WOULD SEE THE FORMAT GO FROM POSSIBILITY TO CLICHE TO DISASTER BY DECEMBER AT ALTAMONT.

FEBRUARY 18, 1971 — CAPITOL THEATER, PORT CHESTER, NEW YORK. FIVE SONGS DEBUTED IN A SINGLE NIGHT: BERTHA, GREATEST STORY EVER TOLD, LOSER, PLAYING IN THE BAND, AND WHARF RAT. NO OTHER SHOW IN THE BAND'S HISTORY INTRODUCED THIS MUCH NEW MATERIAL AT ONCE. SEVERAL BECAME PERMANENT STAPLES PLAYED FOR THE NEXT 24 YEARS. A BOMB THREAT DURING THE NEW RIDERS' OPENING SET FORCED AN EVACUATION — MANAGER SAM CUTLER EMPTIED THE HALL, FIREMEN WALKED DOWN THE AISLES, AND AFTER A BRIEF SEARCH THE SHOW RESUMED. SEVERAL HUNDRED GRINNING CRASHERS STREAMED BACK IN WITHOUT TICKETS. THE DARK STAR THAT NIGHT OPENED INTO WHARF RAT AND BACK — THE UNNAMED IMPROVISATION EMBEDDED IN THAT SEQUENCE LATER EARNED THE NAME "THE BEAUTIFUL JAM" AND WAS INCLUDED ON THE RETROSPECTIVE SO MANY ROADS. PHIL LESH WAS REPORTEDLY MOVED TO TEARS LISTENING TO IT WITH HISTORIAN DAVID GANS DECADES LATER. A GEORGETOWN UNIVERSITY STUDENT NAMED RAY TRIFARI DROVE 250 MILES, TAPED THE SHOW, AND BROADCAST IT ON THE COLLEGE RADIO STATION. HE CALLED THE DEAD "PROBABLY THE BEST AMERICAN BAND." RELEASED ON THE AMERICAN BEAUTY 50TH ANNIVERSARY REISSUE (2020) AS BONUS MATERIAL. SONGS FROM THIS RUN ALSO ON SKULL AND ROSES (1971).

FEBRUARY 19, 1971 — CAPITOL THEATER, PORT CHESTER, NEW YORK. TWO MORE SONGS DEBUTED — BIRD SONG AND DEAL — MAKING SEVEN NEW SONGS IN TWO NIGHTS. BIRD SONG WAS WRITTEN AS A TRIBUTE TO JANIS JOPLIN, WHO HAD DIED OCTOBER 4, 1970. JOPLIN HAD PERFORMED AT THIS SAME THEATER ON AUGUST 8, 1970, AND SHARED DEEP CONNECTIONS WITH THE BAND FROM THE CANADIAN TRAIN TOUR EARLIER THAT YEAR. A HELL'S ANGEL NAMED MARIO PLAYED TAMBOURINE THROUGHOUT THE SET. THOMAS LAMB HAD DESIGNED THE CAPITOL IN 1926 — A 2,000-SEAT MOVIE PALACE WITH A 65-FOOT DOMED CEILING, PRESIDENTIAL BOXES, AND 271 HAND-CARVED SQUIRRELS IN THE MOLDINGS. IT WAS THE ONLY THEATER WITH A REFRIGERATING SYSTEM IN WESTCHESTER COUNTY. PROMOTER HOWARD STEIN HAD TRANSFORMED IT FROM A MOVIE HOUSE INTO WHAT GARCIA CALLED ONE OF ONLY TWO THEATERS WORTH PLAYING — THE OTHER BEING THE FILLMORE EAST. STEIN WAS FIGHTING PORT CHESTER CITY COUNCIL TO KEEP THE PLACE OPEN, BATTLING RESTRAINING ORDERS AND ORDINANCES BANNING LIVE MUSIC AFTER 1 AM. RELEASED AS THREE FROM THE VAULT (2007) — THE COMPLETE SHOW TAKEN FROM ORIGINAL MULTITRACK TAPES. IT HAD BEEN READY TO GO SINCE 1993 BEFORE BEING INEXPLICABLY SHELVED FOR 14 YEARS. SONGS FROM THIS RUN ALSO ON SKULL AND ROSES.

FEBRUARY 20, 1971 — CAPITOL THEATER, PORT CHESTER, NEW YORK. DR. STANLEY KRIPPNER OF THE MAIMONIDES MEDICAL CENTER IN BROOKLYN CONDUCTED A DREAM TELEPATHY EXPERIMENT. A PSYCHIC SUBJECT NAMED MALCOLM BESSENT WAS MONITORED AT THE MAIMONIDES DREAM LABORATORY WITH ELECTRODES, 45 MILES AWAY. AT 11:30 PM, SIX SLIDES OF ART PRINTS WERE PROJECTED ONTO A SCREEN ABOVE THE STAGE — INCLUDING WORKS BY MAGRITTE AND AN IMAGE OF THE SEVEN SPINAL CHAKRAS. THE 2,000-PERSON AUDIENCE SERVED AS "TELEPATHIC TRANSMITTERS," ENCOURAGED TO "SEND" THE IMAGES TO BESSENT AS HE SLEPT. STAFF MEMBER RONNIE MASTRION RANDOMLY SELECTED SLIDES USING COIN TOSSES FROM A POOL OF FOURTEEN. THE RESULTS: WHEN THE CHAKRAS IMAGE WAS THE TARGET, BESSENT DREAMED OF "NATURAL ENERGY," "SOLAR ENERGY," AND "A SPINAL COLUMN." FOR THE MAGRITTE PAINTING — A HEADLESS WOMAN IN A TRANSPARENT GOWN — HE DREAMED OF "A LITTLE GIRL'S DOLL" AND "A FANTASY." BESSENT'S CORRECT TRANSCRIPT-TARGET PAIRS RECEIVED THE HIGHEST RATING FOUR TIMES OUT OF SIX. KRIPPNER CALCULATED "ONLY 12 CHANCES OUT OF 100" THIS COULD OCCUR BY COINCIDENCE. THE RESEARCHERS ACKNOWLEDGED THAT MOST OF THE 2,000 TRANSMITTERS WERE "ALREADY IN AN ALTERED STATE DURING THE CONCERT." WHATEVER YOU BELIEVE ABOUT TELEPATHY, THIS WAS THE ONLY ROCK SHOW EVER USED AS A CONTROLLED SCIENTIFIC EXPERIMENT.

FEBRUARY 21, 1971 — CAPITOL THEATER, PORT CHESTER, NEW YORK. NIGHT FOUR OF SIX — HALFWAY THROUGH THE LONGEST SINGLE-VENUE RUN OF THE YEAR. WHEN A BAND PLAYS THE SAME ROOM SIX NIGHTS IN A ROW, SOMETHING CHANGES. THE ACOUSTICS STOP BEING A SURPRISE. THE PERFORMERS KNOW WHERE THE DEAD SPOTS ARE, WHERE THE SOUND POOLS, HOW THE ROOM RESPONDS. GOOD LOVIN' IN THE SECOND SET STRETCHES TO 17 MINUTES WITH AN EXTENDED DRUMS SECTION THAT TRANSITIONS INTO UNCLE JOHN'S BAND. WITHOUT A SECOND DRUMMER OR A KEYBOARD TO SHARE THE SONIC SPACE, THE FIVE-PIECE SOUND IS FULLY EXPOSED — THERE IS NOWHERE TO HIDE AND NO ONE TO FILL THE GAPS. EVERY INSTRUMENT MATTERS MORE. COLD RAIN AND SNOW OPENS THE NIGHT WITH PURPOSE. THE FIRST SET IS HEAVY WITH RECENT MATERIAL — SONGS FROM WORKINGMAN'S DEAD AND AMERICAN BEAUTY THAT WERE LESS THAN A YEAR OLD, STILL BEING FIGURED OUT IN FRONT OF AN AUDIENCE. EASY WIND FEATURES PIGPEN AT HIS GRITTIEST — A FREIGHT TRAIN OF RHYTHM AND BLUES UNDERNEATH A DOMED CEILING WITH CARVED SQUIRRELS STARING DOWN. NEW RIDERS OF THE PURPLE SAGE OPENED WITH GARCIA ON PEDAL STEEL, THEN HE PICKED UP HIS ELECTRIC GUITAR AND STARTED THE DEAD'S SET WITHOUT LEAVING THE STAGE. SONGS FROM THIS RUN ON SKULL AND ROSES.

FEBRUARY 23, 1971 — CAPITOL THEATER, PORT CHESTER, NEW YORK. NIGHT FIVE OF SIX — BACK FROM A DAY OFF. THE OTHER ONE STRETCHES PAST TWENTY MINUTES WITH LESH'S BASS LEADING THE BAND INTO TERRITORY THAT WOULDN'T HAVE BEEN REACHABLE WITH MORE MUSICIANS ON STAGE. FEBRUARY 24, 1971 — CAPITOL THEATER, PORT CHESTER, NEW YORK. THE FINAL NIGHT. SIX SHOWS IN SEVEN DAYS. BOB WEIR ADDRESSED THE BOMB THREAT FROM OPENING NIGHT, THANKING "WHOEVER IT WAS PHONED IN THAT BOMB REPORT" FOR GETTING IN FREE — A NOD TO THE SEVERAL HUNDRED CRASHERS WHO HAD POURED BACK INTO THE THEATER AFTER THE EVACUATION WITHOUT PAYING. TURN ON YOUR LOVELIGHT CLOSES THE RUN AT OVER 22 MINUTES. GOOD LOVIN' IN THE FIRST SET RUNS NEARLY 17 MINUTES — THE SIGNATURE CLIMAX OF THE WEEK. HARD TO HANDLE SHOWCASED WHAT ONE REVIEWER CALLED "POWERFUL GROOVE AND COHESIVENESS" BETWEEN LESH AND KREUTZMANN. THE BAND DEBUTED SEVEN SONGS IN THE FIRST TWO NIGHTS AND THEN SPENT FOUR MORE NIGHTS BREAKING THEM IN. BY THIS FINAL SHOW, BERTHA AND PLAYING IN THE BAND ALREADY SOUNDED LIKE THEY'D BEEN IN THE REPERTOIRE FOR MONTHS. THE CAPITOL THEATER WAS ADDED TO THE NATIONAL REGISTER OF HISTORIC PLACES IN 1984. DECADES LATER, PETER SHAPIRO REOPENED IT WITH BOB DYLAN ON SEPTEMBER 4, 2012. A BAR NAMED GARCIA'S OPENED INSIDE, WITH THE FAMILY'S APPROVAL. PHIL LESH PERFORMED HIS 100TH SHOW THERE IN MARCH 2023 — A BANNER ABOVE THE ORCHESTRA COMMEMORATED THE OCCASION. SONGS FROM THIS RUN ON SKULL AND ROSES (1971).
1971-04-04

207w
APRIL 4, 1971 — MANHATTAN CENTER, NEW YORK. FIVE THOUSAND PEOPLE CRAMMED INTO A BALLROOM BUILT FOR TWO THOUSAND. THE BAND CALLED IT THEIR "FIRST ANNUAL DANCE MARATHON" — FIVE DOLLAR GENERAL ADMISSION, A PROMISE TO PLAY AS LONG AS THE AUDIENCE DANCED. THE MANHATTAN CENTER'S GRAND BALLROOM SAT ON THE SEVENTH FLOOR OF WHAT HAD BEEN THE MANHATTAN OPERA HOUSE, BUILT IN 1906 BY OSCAR HAMMERSTEIN I TO COMPETE WITH THE METROPOLITAN OPERA BY OFFERING CHEAPER SEATS FOR ORDINARY NEW YORKERS. THE MET FELT SO THREATENED THEY PAID HAMMERSTEIN $1.2 MILLION TO STOP PRODUCING OPERA FOR TEN YEARS — ONE OF THE MOST EXTRAORDINARY BUYOUTS IN PERFORMING ARTS HISTORY. THE SHOW RAN SIX HOURS AND ENDED WITH UNCLE JOHN'S BAND. HALF THE CROWD REMAINED 30 MINUTES AFTER THE FINALE, REFUSING TO BELIEVE IT WAS OVER. A NEW YORK POST CRITIC CLAIMED THE DEAD COULD EASILY FILL MADISON SQUARE GARDEN — THEY CHOSE THIS INSTEAD. BY 4 PM THE STREETS OUTSIDE WERE FLOODED WITH PEOPLE LOOKING FOR TICKETS. IN 1926, WARNER BROTHERS HAD RENTED THIS SAME BALLROOM TO RECORD THE NEW YORK PHILHARMONIC FOR "DON JUAN" — THE FIRST COMMERCIAL FILM WITH A RECORDED MUSICAL SOUNDTRACK. IN 1976, THE UNIFICATION CHURCH BOUGHT THE BUILDING FOR $3 MILLION.

APRIL 5, 1971 — MANHATTAN CENTER, NEW YORK. NIGHT TWO OF THE "DANCE MARATHON." THE OVERCROWDING FROM THE FIRST NIGHT CONTINUED — THE GRAND BALLROOM'S 10,000-SQUARE-FOOT HARDWOOD DANCE FLOOR AND 40-FOOT VAULTED CEILING WITH HAND-PAINTED MURALS WERE DESIGNED BY THE FREEMASONS IN 1922 FOR SOMETHING MORE CEREMONIAL THAN THIS. THE ROOM HAD HOSTED TOMMY DORSEY, LAWRENCE WELK, AND FRANK SINATRA BEFORE THE DEAD TOOK OVER. THE BAND KEPT THEIR PROMISE FROM NIGHT ONE — AS LONG AS THE AUDIENCE DANCED, THEY PLAYED. THE FIVE-PIECE LINEUP WAS AT ITS MOST DIRECT HERE: NO CUSHION OF A SECOND DRUMMER, NO HARMONIC SUPPORT FROM A KEYBOARD, JUST FIVE MUSICIANS FILLING A BALLROOM THAT ONCE HELD THE NEW YORK PHILHARMONIC. THESE MANHATTAN CENTER SHOWS FELL BETWEEN THE CAPITOL THEATER RUN IN FEBRUARY AND THE FILLMORE EAST CLOSING RUN LATER THIS MONTH — THREE DIFFERENT NEW YORK AREA RESIDENCIES IN TEN WEEKS, EACH IN A ROOM WITH ITS OWN HISTORY AND ITS OWN SOUND. THE DEAD COULD HAVE PLAYED MADISON SQUARE GARDEN. THEY CHOSE THREE NIGHTS IN A SEVENTH-FLOOR BALLROOM WHERE PEOPLE WERE STANDING ON EACH OTHER.

APRIL 6, 1971 — MANHATTAN CENTER, NEW YORK. THE FINAL NIGHT OF THE "DANCE MARATHON" AND THE MOST DANGEROUSLY OVERCROWDED. PROMOTER HOWARD STEIN LATER ADMITTED THAT APPROXIMATELY 7,500 PEOPLE WERE IN ATTENDANCE — MORE THAN THREE TIMES THE ROOM'S CAPACITY. SECURITY PERSONNEL WERE ILLEGALLY SELLING TICKETS THROUGH BASEMENT DOORS. COUNTERFEIT TICKETS AND ROOF BREAK-INS COMPOUNDED THE PROBLEM. ONE ATTENDEE REPORTED THAT "WHILE THERE WAS ROOM IN SOME PARTS OF THE ROOM FOR RHYTHMIC BREATHING, DANCING WAS RARELY A POSSIBILITY." THE CROWD WAS ORDERLY DESPITE THE CONDITIONS — PEOPLE LAY ON THE FLOOR WITHOUT BEING TRAMPLED. "THE COAT CHECKROOM HAD NO COAT CHECKER AND WAS RUN ON SOUND ANARCHISTIC PRINCIPLES." A "PLEASANT LOW-KEY TRUST" PREVAILED. ATTENDEES WORE T-SHIRTS, FLANNEL, ARMY JACKETS, OVERALLS. THE AUDIENCE SHOUTED THE LYRICS TO CASEY JONES. STEIN ISSUED A FORMAL APOLOGY ACKNOWLEDGING HE WAS "ENTIRELY" AT FAULT FOR OVERSELLING, WHILE ADDING THAT THE DEAD "PLAYED AS WELL AS I'VE EVER HEARD THEM." THE TRUCKIN' IN THE SECOND SET GOES DEEP — THE KIND OF EXTENDED EXPLORATION THAT ONLY HAPPENS WHEN A BAND HAS BEEN PLAYING THE SAME ROOM FOR THREE NIGHTS AND KNOWS EXACTLY WHAT THE WALLS CAN TAKE.

APRIL 7, 1971 — THE MUSIC HALL, BOSTON. BUILT IN 1852 — ONE OF THE OLDEST CONTINUOUSLY OPERATING THEATERS IN THE UNITED STATES AND THE ORIGINAL HOME OF THE BOSTON SYMPHONY ORCHESTRA. ON THE EVE OF THE EMANCIPATION PROCLAMATION, DECEMBER 31, 1862, NORTHERN ABOLITIONISTS GATHERED IN THIS ROOM TO CELEBRATE AS THE CLOCK STRUCK MIDNIGHT. FREDERICK DOUGLASS WAS THERE. HARRIET TUBMAN WAS THERE. HARRIET BEECHER STOWE, WILLIAM LLOYD GARRISON, AND WENDELL PHILLIPS WERE THERE. THE BUILDING HOUSED AMERICA'S FIRST CONCERT PIPE ORGAN — INSTALLED IN 1862, BUILT IN GERMANY BY E.F. WALCKER AND COMPANY OF LUDWIGSBURG, 5,474 PIPES AND 84 REGISTERS. THE LARGEST IN THE COUNTRY AT THE TIME. A CENTURY LATER, THE DEAD PLAYED THIS SAME ROOM. ST. STEPHEN IN THE SECOND SET LEADS INTO DRUMS AND THEN NOT FADE AWAY — THE LEAN FIVE-PIECE BAND DRIVING HARD WITH KREUTZMANN ALONE BEHIND THE KIT. THE FIRST SET RUNS HEAVY ON RECENTLY WRITTEN MATERIAL, THE BAND STILL TESTING SONGS IN FRONT OF AUDIENCES THAT HADN'T HEARD THEM YET. FOR A VENUE, THE MUSIC HALL NEEDS NO EMBELLISHMENT. ITS HISTORY SPEAKS FOR ITSELF.

APRIL 8, 1971 — THE MUSIC HALL, BOSTON. JON LANDAU, WRITING IN THE BOSTON PHOENIX, HATED IT. HE CALLED THE PERFORMANCE POORLY EXECUTED — THE BAND "PERFORMED LIKE SLOBS" WITH UNMIXED SOUND WHERE "YOU COULDN'T HEAR THE BASS UNTIL HALFWAY THROUGH THE CONCERT." HE CALLED THE INSTRUMENTAL WORK "UNINSPIRED" AND THE ATTITUDE "STERILE AND SANCTIMONIOUS." HE ASKED: "WHO CARES IF THEY PLAY FOR SIX HOURS WHEN FOUR OF THEM ARE LESS THAN ORDINARY?" THIS WAS THE SAME JON LANDAU WHO WOULD LATER WATCH BRUCE SPRINGSTEEN PLAY AND DECLARE "I SAW ROCK AND ROLL FUTURE AND ITS NAME IS BRUCE SPRINGSTEEN." HE WAS FAMOUSLY DEMANDING. THE TAPE TELLS A DIFFERENT STORY. I SECOND THAT EMOTION — A SMOKEY ROBINSON COVER, DEBUTED THIS NIGHT — SITS IN THE FIRST SET. DARK STAR OPENS THE SECOND. GOOD LOVIN' CLOSES IT. LANDAU ACKNOWLEDGED GARCIA'S "EXCEPTIONAL" GUITAR ABILITIES EVEN AS HE PANNED EVERYTHING ELSE. HIS ASSESSMENT CONTRASTED SHARPLY WITH OTHER CONTEMPORARY REVIEWS OF THESE SHOWS, WHICH WERE ENTHUSIASTIC. A BETTY CANTOR RECORDING OF THIS SHOW EXISTS — A CLEAN CAPTURE OF WHAT LANDAU HEARD AND DISMISSED. WHETHER HE WAS RIGHT OR WRONG DEPENDS ON WHAT YOU EXPECT FROM A ROCK BAND IN A 119-YEAR-OLD THEATER.

APRIL 25, 1971 — FILLMORE EAST, NEW YORK. THE DEAD'S FINAL RUN AT BILL GRAHAM'S THEATER ON SECOND AVENUE — FIVE NIGHTS BEGINNING TONIGHT. GRAHAM WAS CLOSING BOTH FILLMORES IN JUNE. THE BUILDING HAD BEEN A YIDDISH THEATER ON THE "JEWISH RIALTO." ANDY WARHOL CONSIDERED TAKING IT OVER FOR THE VELVET UNDERGROUND BUT DECLINED. GRAHAM OPENED IT MARCH 8, 1968 — CAPACITY AROUND 2,700 SEATS, TICKETS NEVER EXCEEDING $5.50. HE CALLED IT "THE CHURCH OF ROCK AND ROLL." THE JOSHUA LIGHT SHOW — A FIVE-PERSON TEAM USING FILM, SLIDE PROJECTORS, COLOR WHEELS, AND OIL ON WATER — PLAYED ABOVE THE STAGE. THE CONCESSION STAND SOLD FRESH FRUIT, BAGELS WITH CREAM CHEESE, AND DANNON YOGURT. RATNER'S KOSHER DELICATESSEN NEXT DOOR SERVED AS GRAHAM'S MAKESHIFT OFFICE. ABOVE THE LOBBY, THE EAST VILLAGE OTHER — AN UNDERGROUND NEWSPAPER "SO COUNTERCULTURAL THAT IT MADE THE VILLAGE VOICE LOOK LIKE A CHURCH CIRCULAR" — OPERATED RENT-FREE UNTIL GRAHAM EVICTED THEM. ALL FIVE NIGHTS WERE RECORDED ON 16-TRACK MULTITRACK. RELEASED AS LADIES AND GENTLEMEN... THE GRATEFUL DEAD (2000) — A 4-CD LIVE ALBUM, 42 SONGS, FOUR HOURS AND 53 MINUTES. CERTIFIED GOLD.

THE BAND'S ARCHIVIST, DAVID LEMIEUX, LATER CALLED THIS SHOW "UNIQUE, SPECIAL, AND INSPIRED," SINGLING OUT THE BIRD SONG AS CONTAINING "SOME OF THE MOST FAMOUS MOMENTS IN GRATEFUL DEAD LIVE HISTORY." THE SECOND-SET HE'S GONE INTO TRUCKIN' ERUPTS INTO A MASSIVE SUITE: TRUCKIN' INTO NOBODY'S FAULT BUT MINE INTO THE OTHER ONE INTO WHARF RAT — THE BAND IN FULL JAZZ MODE, STRETCHING SONGS INTO LONG INSTRUMENTAL CONVERSATIONS WHERE THE DESTINATION MATTERS LESS THAN THE JOURNEY. JUNE 24, 1973 — PORTLAND MEMORIAL COLISEUM, PORTLAND, OR. AN ENGINEERING MARVEL: THE ENTIRE STRUCTURE STANDS ON JUST FOUR MASSIVE CONCRETE PIERS DESPITE BEING DOUBLE THE SIZE OF A CITY BLOCK. EIGHTY THOUSAND SQUARE FEET OF GLASS WINDOWS WRAP THE EXTERIOR — WHEN THE BLACKOUT CURTAINS ARE OPEN, THIS IS POSSIBLY THE ONLY ARENA IN THE WORLD WITH A THREE-HUNDRED-AND-SIXTY-DEGREE VIEW TO THE OUTSIDE. AN ATTENDEE HEARING EYES OF THE WORLD FOR THE FIRST TIME DESCRIBED IT AS "LIKE A SWEET BREEZE BLOWING FROM THE STAGE." THE CHINA DOLL WAS "AMONG THE MOST CONFIDENTLY PLAYED VERSIONS EVER." TWO DAYS AFTER THE VANCOUVER SHOW, THE DARK STAR INTO EYES PAIRING HAD BECOME A SIGNATURE 1973 MOVE — AND BY JUNE IT WAS BEING PERFORMED WITH A DEPTH THE FEBRUARY VERSIONS HADN'T REACHED. JULY 28, 1973 — WATKINS GLEN GRAND PRIX RACEWAY, WATKINS GLEN, NY. SIX HUNDRED THOUSAND PEOPLE. ONE IN EVERY THREE HUNDRED AND FIFTY AMERICANS. THE GUINNESS BOOK OF WORLD RECORDS RECORD FOR LARGEST AUDIENCE AT A POP FESTIVAL — FIFTY PERCENT LARGER THAN WOODSTOCK. THE VILLAGE POPULATION WAS THREE THOUSAND. BY WEDNESDAY — THREE DAYS BEFORE THE SHOW — FIFTY THOUSAND PEOPLE WERE ALREADY THERE. VEHICLES BLOCKED ROADS FOURTEEN MILES OUT. WHAT WAS SUPPOSED TO BE A FRIDAY SOUNDCHECK TURNED INTO A FULL CONCERT: ALL THREE BANDS — THE DEAD, THE ALLMAN BROTHERS, AND THE BAND — PLAYED FOR OVER A HUNDRED THOUSAND PEOPLE WHO HAD ARRIVED EARLY. DURING THE BAND'S SET, A THUNDERSTORM HIT. EVERYONE FLED EXCEPT GARTH HUDSON, WHO STAYED PLUGGED IN AND IMPROVISED ALONE THROUGH THE STORM. THE NEW YORK TIMES DESCRIBED "HALF-NAKED BODIES COATED WITH BROWN SLIME, MOVING RHYTHMICALLY TO THE MUSIC" AND COMPARED THE SCENE TO A BOSCH PAINTING. ROLLING STONE SAID THE SITE AFTERWARD LOOKED LIKE "A WAR HAD BEEN FOUGHT THERE." BUTCH TRUCKS ON THE ALL-BAND ENCORE: "A DISASTER — THE BAND WAS DRUNK, DEAD TRIPPING, ALLMAN BROTHERS COKED OUT." THE DEAD REFUSED COMMERCIAL RELEASE OF THE PERFORMANCE. TWO TEENAGERS — MITCHEL WEISER AND BONNIE BICKWIT — DISAPPEARED HITCHHIKING TO THE FESTIVAL. THEY WERE NEVER FOUND.

NOVEMBER 14, 1973 — SAN DIEGO INTERNATIONAL SPORTS ARENA, SAN DIEGO, CA. THREE DAYS AFTER THE WINTERLAND CLOSING NIGHT, THE DEAD PLAYED ONE OF THE GREAT STRUCTURAL EXPERIMENTS OF 1973. THE OTHER ONE SERVED AS A HOME BASE — THE BAND KEPT LAUNCHING INTO OTHER SONGS AND CIRCLING BACK: TRUCKIN' INTO THE OTHER ONE INTO BIG RIVER INTO THE OTHER ONE INTO EYES OF THE WORLD INTO THE OTHER ONE INTO WHARF RAT. ALMOST NO OTHER SHOWS EXIST WHERE THE OTHER ONE WAS INTERRUPTED TWICE BEFORE RESOLVING. THE TRANSITIONS WORKED — THE GRADUAL DISSOLUTION INTO WHARF RAT WAS CALLED "ESPECIALLY MOVING" AND "TOP OF THE LINE." THE FIRST SET'S CUMBERLAND BLUES WAS PERFORMED AS "A FAST, FREE FOR ALL VERSION" THAT "BARELY HANGS ON TO REALITY." THIS WAS THE BUILDING WHERE HENDRIX PLAYED HIS LAST AMERICAN CONCERT IN 1970 — TWO DIFFERENT APPROACHES TO THE OUTER LIMITS OF WHAT A GUITAR COULD DO IN FRONT OF AN AUDIENCE. THE ARENA'S ARCHITECT, LEONARD VEITZER, USED A TENSION-CABLE SYSTEM TO SUSPEND THE ROOF WITH NO SUPPORT COLUMNS ANYWHERE INSIDE. DONNA JEAN GODCHAUX WAS VERY PREGNANT — HER SON ZION WOULD BE BORN IN JANUARY. THIS SHOW WAS SELECTED AS THE 1973 ENTRY IN THE THIRTY TRIPS AROUND THE SUN BOX SET — ITS SELECTION OVER THE MORE FAMOUS WINTERLAND AND RFK SHOWS SPEAKS FOR ITSELF.
